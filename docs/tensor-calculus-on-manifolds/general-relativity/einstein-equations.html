The Einstein equations

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

Setup

Spacetime is a four-dimensional smooth manifold MMM equipped with a Lorentzian metric g\tens{g}g of signature (−,+,+,+)(-, +, +, +)(−,+,+,+). Test particles follow timelike (massive) or null (massless) of the of g\tens{g}g. The geometry of g\tens{g}g is the gravitational field; the dynamical content of GR is the equation that determines g\tens{g}g from matter content.

The equations

The Einstein field equations are

  Gμν+Λ gμν=8πGc4 Tμν,  \boxed{\; G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\, g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\, T_{\mu\nu}, \;} Gμν​+Λgμν​=c48πG​Tμν​,​

where:

  • Gμν=Rμν−12 R gμνG_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}\, R\, g_{\mu\nu}Gμν​=Rμν​−21​Rgμν​ is the Einstein tensor built from the and scalar curvature of g\tens{g}g.
  • Λ\LambdaΛ is the cosmological constant, a constant observed to be small and positive.
  • GGG is Newton’s gravitational constant and ccc the speed of light; 8πG/c4≈2.08×10−43 N−18\pi G / c^4 \approx 2.08 \times 10^{-43}\, \mathrm{N}^{-1}8πG/c4≈2.08×10−43N−1 in SI units. Most GR work sets c=1c = 1c=1 and G=1G = 1G=1, in which case the prefactor is 8π8\pi8π.
  • TμνT_{\mu\nu}Tμν​ is the of matter and non-gravitational fields. It is a symmetric (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2)-tensor whose components are energy density, momentum density and stress.

Ten components on each side (symmetric 4×44 \times 44×4). The contracted Bianchi identity imposes four identities among the ten equations, and diffeomorphism invariance makes four of the ten components of gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​ gauge: six equations for six components.

Why this combination?

The left-hand side is forced by three requirements:

  1. A symmetric (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2)-tensor. Same shape as TμνT_{\mu\nu}Tμν​.
  2. Built from g\tens{g}g and at most its second derivatives.
  3. Divergence-free. Local conservation of stress–energy ∇μTμν=0\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0∇μTμν​=0 requires the same on the left. The contracted Bianchi identity gives ∇μGμν=0\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0∇μGμν​=0 automatically; ∇μgμν=0\nabla^\mu g_{\mu\nu} = 0∇μgμν​=0 by metric compatibility. So Gμν+Λ gμνG_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\, g_{\mu\nu}Gμν​+Λgμν​ has divergence zero for any Λ\LambdaΛ.

: in $4$D the only tensors meeting all three are a Gμν+b gμνa\, G_{\mu\nu} + b\, g_{\mu\nu}aGμν​+bgμν​. That is essentially the whole derivation: any tensorial, second-order theory of a metric in four dimensions leads to the Einstein equations.

Vacuum and matter

Vacuum. With Tμν=0T_{\mu\nu} = 0Tμν​=0 and Λ=0\Lambda = 0Λ=0:

Gμν=0⟺Rμν=0.G_{\mu\nu} = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad R_{\mu\nu} = 0. Gμν​=0⟺Rμν​=0.

The trace of Gμν=0G_{\mu\nu} = 0Gμν​=0 gives −R=0-R = 0−R=0, so R=0R = 0R=0 and the equation collapses to Rμν=0R_{\mu\nu} = 0Rμν​=0. Vacuum solutions are Ricci-flat Lorentzian 444-manifolds. The Riemann tensor need not vanish: Weyl curvature carries the gravitational degrees of freedom, and Schwarzschild and gravitational waves are non-trivial vacuum solutions.

With matter. The perfect-fluid, electromagnetic and scalar-field stress–energy tensors are on the stress–energy page. For each, ∇μTμν=0\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0∇μTμν​=0 follows from the matter field equations, consistent with the contracted Bianchi identity on the left.

Geometric content, briefly

  • The Ricci tensor RμνR_{\mu\nu}Rμν​ measures how the volume of a small ball of freely falling test particles changes: positive RμνuμuνR_{\mu\nu} u^\mu u^\nuRμν​uμuν focuses it, negative defocuses it. Geodesic deviation is the precise statement: Riemann drives the relative acceleration of nearby geodesics, and Ricci is its trace.
  • The Weyl tensor measures tidal distortion at fixed volume: the trace-free, shearing part of the curvature.
  • A vacuum solution has all curvature in the Weyl tensor: Ricci vanishes, so a small ball initially at rest keeps its volume to leading order, but tidal distortion remains.

Together with the geodesic equation for free particles, this is a theory of gravity: matter tells spacetime how to curve, and spacetime tells matter how to move.

Lovelock's theorem

In four dimensions, every symmetric (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2)-tensor that is (i) built from the metric and at most its first and second derivatives and (ii) divergence-free is a linear combination

a Gμν+b gμνa\, G_{\mu\nu} + b\, g_{\mu\nu} aGμν​+bgμν​

of the Einstein tensor and the metric. So the left-hand side of the Einstein equations — including the cosmological-constant term — is forced, not chosen, once a tensorial second-order theory of the metric is demanded. In dimension n>4n > 4n>4, additional “Lovelock terms” (Gauss–Bonnet and higher) become available, which is one reason higher-dimensional gravity theories are less rigid than $4$D GR.

Compact support

A function (or vector field, or differential form) has compact support if it vanishes outside some compact subset K⊆MK \subseteq MK⊆M. On a non-compact manifold this is the condition that makes ∫M\int_M∫M​ converge and that kills the boundary contributions at infinity — it is why Stokes’s theorem and the integration of nnn-forms are stated for compactly supported forms. On a compact manifold every smooth function has compact support automatically, so the qualifier can be dropped.

Lie group

A Lie group is a group that is also a smooth manifold, with multiplication and inversion smooth maps. Examples: GLn(R)GL_n(\mathbb{R})GLn​(R), the rotation groups SO(n)SO(n)SO(n), the Poincaré group of , Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn under addition, the circle S1S^1S1. Embedded manifold

A smooth submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN is a subset M⊆RNM \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NM⊆RN that is smoothly parametrized near each point: around every p∈Mp \in Mp∈M there is an open U⊆RNU \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NU⊆RN, an open V⊆RnV \subseteq \mathbb{R}^nV⊆Rn, and a smooth map Φ:V→RN\Phi: V \to \mathbb{R}^NΦ:V→RN with everywhere-injective differential that is a homeomorphism onto U∩MU \cap MU∩M. The image Φ(V)\Phi(V)Φ(V) is an open piece of MMM, and the nnn coordinates on VVV are a chart on MMM.

The embedded view of differential geometry takes advantage of the ambient RN\mathbb{R}^NRN: tangent vectors are vectors in RN\mathbb{R}^NRN (spanning the tangent plane to MMM); the metric is the pullback of the Euclidean inner product; integration is over an embedded submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN. The Whitney embedding theorem says every smooth nnn-manifold can be embedded in R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n, so the embedded view loses no generality in principle. In practice the ambient space is excess baggage — isometric embeddings need many more dimensions (Nash), and a Lorentzian metric can only be induced from an indefinite-signature ambient space — which is why the abstract view is the working one.
