Einstein–Cartan — gravity with torsion

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

The is the unique -free metric-compatible connection. Drop the torsion-free assumption and you get Einstein–Cartan theory: a metric-compatible (but not torsion-free) connection on spacetime, with torsion sourced by the spin density of matter. EC gravity agrees with standard GR wherever spin density is negligible — everywhere short of densities far beyond nuclear — and is the natural setting when fermion spin is allowed to source geometry.

Field equations

Varying with respect to g\tens{g}g and Γ\GammaΓ as independent fields (the Palatini variation) gives two equations:

Einstein-like equation.

Gμν[Γ]=8πGc4 Tμν,G_{\mu\nu}[\Gamma] = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\, T_{\mu\nu}, Gμν​[Γ]=c48πG​Tμν​,

identical in form to GR but with the built from the torsionful connection Γ\GammaΓ and a generalized on the right.

Cartan equation (the torsion equation).

Tρμν+δμρ Tσνσ−δνρ Tσμσ=8πGc4 Sρμν,T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} + \delta^\rho_\mu\, T^\sigma{}_{\nu\sigma} - \delta^\rho_\nu\, T^\sigma{}_{\mu\sigma} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\, S^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}, Tρμν​+δμρ​Tσνσ​−δνρ​Tσμσ​=c48πG​Sρμν​,

where SρμνS^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}Sρμν​ is the spin density tensor of matter — the antisymmetric-in-μν\mu\nuμν object built from the spin angular momentum carried by the matter fields. Specifically, for Dirac fermions ψ\psiψ, the spin density is bilinear in ψ\psiψ and ψˉ\bar\psiψˉ​.

The Cartan equation is algebraic — there are no derivatives of torsion on the left — so torsion is not a propagating field. It vanishes wherever the spin density vanishes (free space, electromagnetic fields, etc.) and is non-zero only inside spinning matter. There are no Einstein–Cartan analogues of gravitational waves carrying torsion.

Comparison with GR

Where spin density vanishes, Einstein–Cartan reduces exactly to GR — same metric, same predictions, same Schwarzschild solution outside. The differences live inside spinning matter:

  • Spinning fluids and dust. Macroscopic spin alignment is rare; in cosmological-fluid models EC is effectively GR.
  • Dense fermionic matter. Inside a neutron star, fermion spins give a non-zero spin density, but the effect is tiny. Because the Cartan equation is algebraic, torsion enters the effective Einstein equations as a term quadratic in the spin density, with each factor carrying an ℏ\hbarℏ. That term rivals ordinary stress–energy only at densities far beyond nuclear.
  • Cosmological singularities. Some EC models avoid the initial singularity that standard GR predicts, replacing it with a bounce — torsion contributes an effective repulsive term at extreme density.

Beyond these, EC is observationally indistinguishable from GR with current data.

Why include it

Einstein–Cartan is a tiny numerical correction to GR, but it earns its place for three reasons:

  1. Conceptual clean-up. The “extra” degree of freedom of the connection — beyond what the metric determines — is what spinors couple to. Standard GR with spinors requires a tetrad/spin-connection formulation; EC makes the splitting natural.
  2. Coupling to fermions. In EC, Dirac fermions couple minimally to the full connection, so their spin sources torsion and they feel it; gauge bosons, whose field strength F=dA\tens{F} = d\tens{A}F=dA needs no connection, neither source nor feel it.
  3. Mathematical generality. The non-torsion-free case is the “generic” affine connection. Treating it as a special case obscures what the metric assumption (Levi-Civita) is doing.

The further story — vielbein/tetrad formulation, spin connection, the action written as a polynomial in differential forms — is the , a separate (longer) topic; the notes page has a sketch.

Spinor

A field that transforms under the Spin group — the double cover of the rotation group SO(n)SO(n)SO(n) (or, in Lorentzian signature, of the Lorentz group) — rather than under the group itself. The double-cover subtlety is physical: a spinor changes sign under a 2π2\pi2π rotation and returns to itself only after 4π4\pi4π. Electrons, quarks, and all Standard-Model fermions are described by (Dirac) spinors. Spin representations do not extend to GLnGL_nGLn​, so spinors cannot be defined from a coordinate basis alone — curved-spacetime spinors need an orthonormal frame and spin connection (the ). Their intrinsic angular momentum is the spin density that sources torsion in Einstein–Cartan gravity.

Cartan formalism

The tetrad (or vielbein) formulation of gravity. Replace the coordinate basis with an orthonormal coframe: nnn one-forms eae^aea with

g=ηab  ea⊗eb,\tens{g} = \eta_{ab}\; e^a \otimes e^b, g=ηab​ea⊗eb,

where η\tens{\eta}η is the flat metric of the appropriate signature (“tetrad” when n=4n = 4n=4). The connection becomes the spin connection ωab\omega^a{}_bωab​, a matrix of one-forms, and torsion and curvature become the two-forms of the Cartan structure equations

Ta=dea+ωab∧eb,Rab=dωab+ωac∧ωcb.T^a = de^a + \omega^a{}_b \wedge e^b, \qquad R^a{}_b = d\omega^a{}_b + \omega^a{}_c \wedge \omega^c{}_b. Ta=dea+ωab​∧eb,Rab​=dωab​+ωac​∧ωcb​.

The payoff: fields on curved spacetime become definable (spinors transform under local frame rotations, which the coordinate-basis formalism has no handle on), and the Einstein–Cartan action becomes a polynomial in differential forms. Misner–Thorne–Wheeler (chapter 13) and Nakahara develop the formalism in full.

Lovelock's theorem

In four dimensions, every symmetric (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2)-tensor that is (i) built from the metric and at most its first and second derivatives and (ii) divergence-free is a linear combination

a Gμν+b gμνa\, G_{\mu\nu} + b\, g_{\mu\nu} aGμν​+bgμν​

of the Einstein tensor and the metric. So the left-hand side of the Einstein equations — including the cosmological-constant term — is forced, not chosen, once a tensorial second-order theory of the metric is demanded. In dimension n>4n > 4n>4, additional “Lovelock terms” (Gauss–Bonnet and higher) become available, which is one reason higher-dimensional gravity theories are less rigid than $4$D GR.
