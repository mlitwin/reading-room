The symplectic form

Minkowski space

The spacetime of special relativity: R4\mathbb{R}^4R4 with the global flat Lorentzian metric

η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2\tens{\eta} = -dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2 η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2

(signature (−,+,+,+)(-, +, +, +)(−,+,+,+)). All Christoffel symbols vanish in these coordinates, so are straight lines and the Riemann tensor is zero — the “no gravity” special case that curved solutions approach asymptotically (Schwarzschild as r→∞r \to \inftyr→∞). Its group is the Poincaré group: translations plus Lorentz transformations. Every tangent space of a Lorentzian manifold is a copy of Minkowski space in miniature — the content of the Part I aside.

Compact support

A function (or vector field, or differential form) has compact support if it vanishes outside some compact subset K⊆MK \subseteq MK⊆M. On a non-compact manifold this is the condition that makes ∫M\int_M∫M​ converge and that kills the boundary contributions at infinity — it is why Stokes’s theorem and the integration of nnn-forms are stated for compactly supported forms. On a compact manifold every smooth function has compact support automatically, so the qualifier can be dropped.

Immersion

A smooth map F:M→NF: M \to NF:M→N is an immersion if its differential dFp:TpM→TF(p)NdF_p: T_p M \to T_{F(p)} NdFp​:Tp​M→TF(p)​N is injective at every point. Embedded manifold

A smooth submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN is a subset M⊆RNM \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NM⊆RN that is smoothly parametrized near each point: around every p∈Mp \in Mp∈M there is an open U⊆RNU \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NU⊆RN, an open V⊆RnV \subseteq \mathbb{R}^nV⊆Rn, and a smooth map Φ:V→RN\Phi: V \to \mathbb{R}^NΦ:V→RN with everywhere-injective differential that is a homeomorphism onto U∩MU \cap MU∩M. The image Φ(V)\Phi(V)Φ(V) is an open piece of MMM, and the nnn coordinates on VVV are a chart on MMM.

The embedded view of differential geometry takes advantage of the ambient RN\mathbb{R}^NRN: tangent vectors are vectors in RN\mathbb{R}^NRN (spanning the tangent plane to MMM); the metric is the pullback of the Euclidean inner product; integration is over an embedded submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN. The Whitney embedding theorem says every smooth nnn-manifold can be embedded in R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n, so the embedded view loses no generality in principle. In practice the ambient space is excess baggage — isometric embeddings need many more dimensions (Nash), and a Lorentzian metric can only be induced from an indefinite-signature ambient space — which is why the abstract view is the working one.
