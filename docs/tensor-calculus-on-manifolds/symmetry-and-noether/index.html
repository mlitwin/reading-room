Symmetry and Noether's theorem

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

Each continuous symmetry of a Lagrangian gives a quantity conserved along its solutions (Noether, 1918). In the language of Part I, a continuous symmetry is the of a vector field ξ\tens{\xi}ξ on configuration space. Its conserved charge pairs the momentum covector with the generator: Jξ=pi ξiJ_\xi = p_i\, \xi^iJξ​=pi​ξi. Where this lands in GR. A Killing vector field of spacetime is a symmetry of the geodesic Lagrangian, and its Noether charge ξμx˙μ\xi_\mu \dot x^\muξμ​x˙μ is conserved along every free-fall path (chapter 8). The energy and angular momentum of Schwarzschild orbits are the two charges that reduce the orbit problem to one dimension.

Compact support

A function (or vector field, or differential form) has compact support if it vanishes outside some compact subset K⊆MK \subseteq MK⊆M. On a non-compact manifold this is the condition that makes ∫M\int_M∫M​ converge and that kills the boundary contributions at infinity — it is why Stokes’s theorem and the integration of nnn-forms are stated for compactly supported forms. On a compact manifold every smooth function has compact support automatically, so the qualifier can be dropped.
