Aside: a metric at a point

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

Everything in Part I works on a bare manifold — no lengths, no angles, no causal structure. This page is the one preview of the structure Part II adds, at the level where it is pure linear algebra: a at a single point. Nothing in chapters 2–5 uses this page. It sits here because the previous page raises exactly the question it answers — vectors and covectors are dual but not identified — and because its Lorentzian case is where special relativity lives.

This is linear algebra on one vector space. No smoothness, no neighborhood of ppp, no structure of MMM beyond TpMT_p MTp​M itself is involved.

Signature and causal structure

By Sylvester’s law of inertia, a basis of TpMT_p MTp​M can always be chosen in which [gμν][g_{\mu\nu}][gμν​] is diagonal with entries ±1\pm 1±1, and the number of each sign — the signature — is independent of the choice. Two cases matter in this book:

  • Riemannian, signature (+,⋯ ,+)(+, \cdots, +)(+,⋯,+): gpg_pgp​ is positive-definite, an ordinary inner product. Every non-zero vector has a positive length gp(v,v)\sqrt{g_p(v, v)}gp​(v,v)​, and angles are defined.
  • Lorentzian, signature (−,+,⋯ ,+)(-, +, \cdots, +)(−,+,⋯,+): the tangent space acquires causal structure. A vector is timelike if gp(v,v)<0g_p(v, v) < 0gp​(v,v)<0, null if gp(v,v)=0g_p(v, v) = 0gp​(v,v)=0, spacelike if gp(v,v)>0g_p(v, v) > 0gp​(v,v)>0. The null vectors form a double cone — the lightcone — with the timelike vectors inside it and the spacelike vectors outside.

In relativity, TpMT_p MTp​M with a Lorentzian gpg_pgp​ is the local inertial frame at the event ppp: 4-velocities of massive particles are timelike, light rays are null, and everything special relativity says about one observer at one event is linear algebra in this single tangent space. Gravity — curvature, dynamics — enters only when gpg_pgp​ is allowed to vary with ppp.

Minkowski space

The spacetime of special relativity: R4\mathbb{R}^4R4 with the global flat Lorentzian metric

η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2\eta = -dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2 η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2

Its group is the Poincaré group: translations plus Lorentz transformations. Every tangent space of a Lorentzian manifold is a copy of Minkowski space in miniature — the content of the Part I aside.

Compact support

A function (or vector field, or differential form) has compact support if it vanishes outside some compact subset K⊆MK \subseteq MK⊆M. On a non-compact manifold this is the condition that makes ∫M\int_M∫M​ converge and that kills the boundary contributions at infinity — it is why Stokes’s theorem and the integration of nnn-forms are stated for compactly supported forms. Embedded manifold

A smooth submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN is a subset M⊆RNM \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NM⊆RN that locally looks like the graph of a smooth function: around every p∈Mp \in Mp∈M there is an open U⊆RNU \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NU⊆RN, an open V⊆RnV \subseteq \mathbb{R}^nV⊆Rn, and a smooth map Φ:V→RN\Phi: V \to \mathbb{R}^NΦ:V→RN with everywhere-injective differential that is a homeomorphism onto U∩MU \cap MU∩M. The image Φ(V)\Phi(V)Φ(V) is an open piece of MMM, and the nnn coordinates on VVV are a chart on MMM.

The embedded view of differential geometry takes advantage of the ambient RN\mathbb{R}^NRN: tangent vectors are vectors in RN\mathbb{R}^NRN (spanning the tangent plane to MMM); the metric is the pullback of the Euclidean inner product; integration is over an embedded submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN. The Whitney embedding theorem says every smooth nnn-manifold can be embedded in R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n, so the embedded view loses no generality in principle. In practice the ambient space is excess baggage — isometric embeddings need many more dimensions (Nash), and a Lorentzian metric can only be induced from an indefinite-signature ambient space — which is why the abstract view is the working one.
