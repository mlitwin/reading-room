The kinetic metric

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

Kinetic energy is quadratic in the velocities. A quadratic form on each tangent space is a metric, so a mechanical system’s kinetic energy is a metric on its configuration space.

For NNN particles in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3, the kinetic energy is

T=12∑AmA ∣r˙A∣2=12 gij q˙iq˙j,g=∑AmA(dxA2+dyA2+dzA2),T = \tfrac12 \sum_A m_A\, \lvert \dot{\tens{r}}_A \rvert^2 = \tfrac12\, g_{ij}\, \dot q^i \dot q^j, \qquad \tens{g} = \sum_A m_A \bigl(dx_A^2 + dy_A^2 + dz_A^2\bigr), T=21​A∑​mA​∣r˙A​∣2=21​gij​q˙​iq˙​j,g=A∑​mA​(dxA2​+dyA2​+dzA2​),

a flat, mass-weighted Euclidean on Q=R3NQ = \mathbb{R}^{3N}Q=R3N. In other coordinates the same metric has other components. In polar coordinates on R2\mathbb{R}^2R2, g=m (dr2+r2 dφ2)\tens{g} = m\,(dr^2 + r^2\, d\varphi^2)g=m(dr2+r2dφ2).

In general, a natural system is a manifold QQQ with a Riemannian metric g\tens{g}g (the kinetic metric) and a function VVV, with

L=12 g(q˙,q˙)−V(q)=12 gij(q) q˙iq˙j−V(q).L = \tfrac12\, \tens{g}(\dot q, \dot q) - V(q) = \tfrac12\, g_{ij}(q)\, \dot q^i \dot q^j - V(q). L=21​g(q˙​,q˙​)−V(q)=21​gij​(q)q˙​iq˙​j−V(q).

Positive kinetic energy is what makes g\tens{g}g Riemannian. A rigid body is the classic case with a non-flat kinetic metric: Q=R3×SO(3)Q = \mathbb{R}^3 \times SO(3)Q=R3×SO(3), with a left-invariant metric on SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) given by the inertia tensor (chapter 12).

The rest of the chapter, and chapters 11–12, take a natural system as the standing example:

  • the Euler–Lagrange equations become ∇q˙q˙=−grad⁡V\nabla_{\dot q} \dot q = -\operatorname{grad} V∇q˙​​q˙​=−gradV (page 4);
  • the momentum is p=q˙♭p = \dot q^\flatp=q˙​♭ and the Hamiltonian is 12 gijpipj+V\tfrac12\, g^{ij} p_i p_j + V21​gijpi​pj​+V (chapter 11);
  • a holonomic constraint replaces g\tens{g}g by an induced metric (page 5);
  • symmetries are Killing fields of g\tens{g}g that preserve VVV (chapter 12).

Lorentzian kinetic metrics. Positive kinetic energy makes g\tens{g}g Riemannian. A relativistic particle has a Lorentzian “kinetic metric”, spacetime’s own g\tens{g}g, and no potential. Free fall is then its geodesic motion (chapter 13).

Tensor density

A tensor density of weight www is a component array that transforms by the times an extra factor [det⁡(∂x′/∂x)]w\bigl[\det(\partial x'/\partial x)\bigr]^w[det(∂x′/∂x)]w of the Jacobian determinant. Weight 000 is an ordinary tensor. The symbol (lower indices) has weight +1+1+1; ∣det⁡g∣\sqrt{|\det g|}∣detg∣​ is a scalar density of weight −1-1−1; their product is an honest tensor — the general pattern, since multiplying by the appropriate power of ∣det⁡g∣\sqrt{|\det g|}∣detg∣​ converts any density to a tensor.
