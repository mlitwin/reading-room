Newtonian mechanics

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

Newton’s framework is the flat special case of this part: NNN point particles in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3, with masses mA>0m_A > 0mA​>0 and positions rA(t)\tens{r}_A(t)rA​(t), A=1,…,NA = 1, \ldots, NA=1,…,N. Laws

N1 (inertia). Read as a postulate: inertial frames exist. In them a particle subject to no net force moves with constant velocity, and N2 holds.

N2. For a particle with momentum pA=mAr˙A\tens{p}_A = m_A \dot{\tens{r}}_ApA​=mA​r˙A​ and net force FA\tens{F}_AFA​,

p˙A=FA,so mAr¨A=FA for constant mass.\dot{\tens{p}}_A = \tens{F}_A, \qquad \text{so } m_A \ddot{\tens{r}}_A = \tens{F}_A \text{ for constant mass.} p˙​A​=FA​,so mA​r¨A​=FA​ for constant mass.

These are 3N3N3N second-order ODEs, or a first-order system on R3N×R3N\mathbb{R}^{3N} \times \mathbb{R}^{3N}R3N×R3N. Positions and velocities at one instant determine the motion (Picard–Lindelöf, for Lipschitz forces).

N3 (action and reaction). The force of AAA on BBB is minus the force of BBB on AAA. The strong form adds that both act along rA−rB\tens{r}_A - \tens{r}_BrA​−rB​. Magnetic forces between moving charges violate it.

Constraints

A f(q,t)=0f(q, t) = 0f(q,t)=0 confines the motion to a submanifold of QQQ. A rigid rod, a bead on a wire and a particle on a sphere are examples. A nonholonomic constraint restricts velocities without reducing the dimension of QQQ; a disk rolling without slipping is the standard case. The Lagrangian formalism works on the constraint submanifold directly (page 3).

Contractible

Two smooth maps F,G:M→NF, G: M \to NF,G:M→N are (smoothly) homotopic, written F≃GF \simeq GF≃G, if one can be deformed into the other: there is a smooth H:M×[0,1]→NH: M \times [0, 1] \to NH:M×[0,1]→N with H(⋅,0)=FH(\cdot, 0) = FH(⋅,0)=F and H(⋅,1)=GH(\cdot, 1) = GH(⋅,1)=G. A manifold MMM is contractible if its identity map is homotopic to a constant map — MMM can be continuously shrunk to a point. Convex and subsets of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn are contractible; Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn is, but Rn∖{0}\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}Rn∖{0} and the sphere are not.

De Rham cohomology is a homotopy invariant: homotopic maps induce the same map on cohomology, so a contractible manifold has the cohomology of a point (all reduced groups vanish — the Poincaré lemma).

Star-shaped

An open set U⊆RnU \subseteq \mathbb{R}^nU⊆Rn is star-shaped about p∈Up \in Up∈U if for every q∈Uq \in Uq∈U, the line segment {(1−t)p+tq:t∈[0,1]}\{(1-t)p + tq : t \in [0, 1]\}{(1−t)p+tq:t∈[0,1]} from ppp to qqq lies entirely in UUU.
