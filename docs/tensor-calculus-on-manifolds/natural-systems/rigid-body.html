The rigid body

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

A rigid body is the standard natural system whose kinetic metric is not flat. Its configuration space is a Lie group, and the motion is geodesic for a metric built from the inertia tensor. Notation on this page follows Marsden–Ratiu: ω\tens{\omega}ω and Ω\tens{\Omega}Ω are angular velocities, not forms.

Euler’s equations

These are Euler’s equations. They are first-order equations in the body frame, solved before reconstructing R(t)R(t)R(t) from R˙=RΩ^\dot R = R\hat{\tens{\Omega}}R˙=RΩ^. Two quantities are conserved: the energy T=12 Π⋅ΩT = \tfrac12\, \tens{\Pi} \cdot \tens{\Omega}T=21​Π⋅Ω and ∣Π∣2=∣π∣2\lvert \tens{\Pi} \rvert^2 = \lvert \tens{\pi} \rvert^2∣Π∣2=∣π∣2. Each solution moves along an intersection of an energy ellipsoid with an angular-momentum sphere. Rotation about the largest or smallest principal axis is stable; rotation about the middle axis is unstable (the tennis-racket theorem).

Euler’s equations are the prototype of reduction by symmetry. Geodesic flow on a Lie group with a left-invariant metric, written in body variables, gives equations on the Lie algebra alone (the Euler–Arnold equations). The same structure describes ideal fluid flow, with SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) replaced by the group of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms.

The heavy top
