  • Lengths. ∣∂θ∣=1|\partial_\theta| = 1∣∂θ​∣=1 at every point; ∣∂φ∣=sin⁡θ|\partial_\varphi| = \sin\theta∣∂φ​∣=sinθ, vanishing at the poles where the chart breaks down.

Why this chart is the “easy” one

In the standard chart, almost everything has a diagonal form:

  • The round metric has the diagonal matrix [gμν]=diag(1,sin⁡2θ)[g_{\mu\nu}] = \mathrm{diag}(1, \sin^2\theta)[gμν​]=diag(1,sin2θ).
  • The basis is orthogonal, so multiplies each component by gμμg_{\mu\mu}gμμ​ or 1/gμμ1/g_{\mu\mu}1/gμμ​ (no sum).
  • The (chapter 8) have only three non-zero entries.

Derivation

For the commutative algebra A=C∞(M)A = C^\infty(M)A=C∞(M) of smooth functions on a manifold, a derivation at p∈Mp \in Mp∈M is an R\mathbb{R}R-linear map D:A→RD: A \to \mathbb{R}D:A→R satisfying the Leibniz rule

Every derivation at ppp is determined by its values on a coordinate system around ppp — D(xi)D(x^i)D(xi) for the chart x=(x1,…,xn)x = (x^1, \ldots, x^n)x=(x1,…,xn) — and the space of derivations is an nnn-dimensional real vector space, isomorphic to TpMT_p MTp​M.

The derivation viewpoint generalizes: a derivation of AAA (with values in AAA) is a vector field; the Lie bracket of vector fields is the commutator of derivations.
