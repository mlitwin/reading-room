A (1, 1)-tensor on the sphere

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

A (1,1)(1, 1)(1,1)-tensor on S2S^2S2 worked out in components: rotation by 90∘90^\circ90∘ in each tangent plane.

Definition and components

In the spherical chart (θ,φ)(\theta, \varphi)(θ,φ), define J\tens{J}J by

J(∂θ)=1sin⁡θ ∂φ,J(∂φ)=−sin⁡θ ∂θ.\tens{J}(\partial_\theta) = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}\, \partial_\varphi, \qquad \tens{J}(\partial_\varphi) = -\sin\theta\, \partial_\theta. J(∂θ​)=sinθ1​∂φ​,J(∂φ​)=−sinθ∂θ​.

At each point J\tens{J}J is a linear map TpS2→TpS2T_p S^2 \to T_p S^2Tp​S2→Tp​S2, so it is a (1,1)(1, 1)(1,1)-tensor field (chapter 2). Its components are

Jθφ=−sin⁡θ,Jφθ=1sin⁡θ,Jθθ=Jφφ=0.J^\theta{}_\varphi = -\sin\theta, \qquad J^\varphi{}_\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}, \qquad J^\theta{}_\theta = J^\varphi{}_\varphi = 0. Jθφ​=−sinθ,Jφθ​=sinθ1​,Jθθ​=Jφφ​=0.

  • Rotation. ∂θ\partial_\theta∂θ​ and ∂φ/sin⁡θ\partial_\varphi / \sin\theta∂φ​/sinθ form an orthonormal basis of the round metric, and J\tens{J}J turns the first into the second and the second into minus the first. So J\tens{J}J is rotation by 90∘90^\circ90∘, and g(Jv,Jw)=g(v,w)\tens{g}(\tens{J}\tens{v}, \tens{J}\tens{w}) = \tens{g}(\tens{v}, \tens{w})g(Jv,Jw)=g(v,w).
  • Complex structure. J2=−id\tens{J}^2 = -\mathrm{id}J2=−id, so J\tens{J}J is an almost complex structure. It extends smoothly over the poles to all of S2S^2S2.
  • Traceless. tr J=Jμμ=0\mathrm{tr}\, \tens{J} = J^\mu{}_\mu = 0trJ=Jμμ​=0. The trace of a (1,1)(1, 1)(1,1)-tensor is a scalar, so this holds in every chart.

Without a metric. The definition above uses only the chart. The metric is what explains it: J\tens{J}J is the rotation determined by the round metric and the orientation.

In the stereographic chart

The carries the components to any other chart. In the stereographic chart, where the round metric is a multiple of dx2+dy2dx^2 + dy^2dx2+dy2, they are constant:

J(∂x)=∂y,J(∂y)=−∂x.\tens{J}(\partial_x) = \partial_y, \qquad \tens{J}(\partial_y) = -\partial_x. J(∂x​)=∂y​,J(∂y​)=−∂x​.

Same tensor, different components. A direct computation in the new chart agrees with the transformed components, which is what makes J\tens{J}J a feature of the sphere rather than of a chart.
