Skew coordinates

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

The skew chart in this book is defined so its grid is visibly tilted relative to the standard chart — the longitude circles are sheared, the latitude circles stay horizontal. (See the diagram on the previous page.) The tilt parameter is fixed throughout: α=π/8≈22.5°\alpha = \pi/8 \approx 22.5°α=π/8≈22.5°.

The simplest definition that gives that picture keeps the standard θ\thetaθ and shears the longitude:

θ~:=θ,φ~:=φ+α cos⁡θ.\tilde\theta := \theta, \qquad \tilde\varphi := \varphi + \alpha\, \cos\theta. θ~:=θ,φ~​:=φ+αcosθ.

The shift by αcos⁡θ\alpha\cos\thetaαcosθ is largest at the poles and zero at the equator, producing the visible tilt of the longitude curves. The Jacobian of (θ,φ)↦(θ~,φ~)(\theta, \varphi) \mapsto (\tilde\theta, \tilde\varphi)(θ,φ)↦(θ~,φ~​) is everywhere non-singular (det⁡=1\det = 1det=1), so this is a valid chart wherever the standard one is.

In the embedded picture,

Φ~(θ~,φ~)=Φ(θ~, φ~−αcos⁡θ~)=(sin⁡θ~ cos⁡ψ,  sin⁡θ~ sin⁡ψ,  cos⁡θ~),ψ:=φ~−αcos⁡θ~.\tilde\Phi(\tilde\theta, \tilde\varphi) = \Phi\big(\tilde\theta,\, \tilde\varphi - \alpha\cos\tilde\theta\big) = (\sin\tilde\theta\, \cos\psi,\; \sin\tilde\theta\, \sin\psi,\; \cos\tilde\theta), \quad \psi := \tilde\varphi - \alpha\cos\tilde\theta. Φ~(θ~,φ~​)=Φ(θ~,φ~​−αcosθ~)=(sinθ~cosψ,sinθ~sinψ,cosθ~),ψ:=φ~​−αcosθ~.

Dual basis

The dual basis dθ~,dφ~d\tilde\theta, d\tilde\varphidθ~,dφ~​ is defined by dθ~(∂θ~)=1d\tilde\theta(\partial_{\tilde\theta}) = 1dθ~(∂θ~​)=1, dθ~(∂φ~)=0d\tilde\theta(\partial_{\tilde\varphi}) = 0dθ~(∂φ~​​)=0, etc. Drawn as vectors via the metric (see below), each dual covector is perpendicular to the other coordinate basis vector, because dθ~(∂φ~)=dφ~(∂θ~)=0d\tilde\theta(\partial_{\tilde\varphi}) = d\tilde\varphi(\partial_{\tilde\theta}) = 0dθ~(∂φ~​​)=dφ~​(∂θ~​)=0. In an orthogonal chart that makes each parallel to its own basis vector; in a non-orthogonal chart it does not:

  • dθ~⊥∂φ~d\tilde\theta \perp \partial_{\tilde\varphi}dθ~⊥∂φ~​​, so dθ~d\tilde\thetadθ~ is not parallel to ∂θ~\partial_{\tilde\theta}∂θ~​.
  • dφ~⊥∂θ~d\tilde\varphi \perp \partial_{\tilde\theta}dφ~​⊥∂θ~​, so dφ~d\tilde\varphidφ~​ is not parallel to ∂φ~\partial_{\tilde\varphi}∂φ~​​.

Talking about covectors’ lengths and angles at all — and drawing them as arrows in the same plane as the basis vectors — means identifying each covector with a vector through the metric, i.e. applying the ♯\sharp♯. Done honestly, the angle between dθ~d\tilde\thetadθ~ and dφ~d\tilde\varphidφ~​ is not π/2\pi/2π/2 either — it is the supplement of the angle between ∂θ~\partial_{\tilde\theta}∂θ~​ and ∂φ~\partial_{\tilde\varphi}∂φ~​​. Angles between covectors use the inverse metric, and inverting a 2×22 \times 22×2 matrix flips the sign of the off-diagonal entry (gθ~φ~=−gθ~φ~/det⁡gg^{\tilde\theta\tilde\varphi} = -g_{\tilde\theta\tilde\varphi}/\det ggθ~φ~​=−gθ~φ~​​/detg), so the cosine flips sign: basis vectors at ≈70°\approx 70°≈70° put the dual covectors at ≈110°\approx 110°≈110°.

Tensor field

A tensor field of type (r,s)(r, s)(r,s) on a smooth manifold MMM is a smooth section of the bundle whose fiber at p∈Mp \in Mp∈M is

TpM⊗r⊗Tp∗M⊗s.T_p M^{\otimes r} \otimes T^*_p M^{\otimes s}. Tp​M⊗r⊗Tp∗​M⊗s.

Equivalently, a C∞(M)C^\infty(M)C∞(M)-multilinear map taking rrr smooth 111-forms and sss smooth vector fields and returning a smooth real-valued function. Vector fields are (1,0)(1, 0)(1,0)-tensor fields; 111-forms are (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1)-tensor fields; kkk-forms are alternating (0,k)(0, k)(0,k)-tensor fields; the metric is a symmetric (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2)-tensor field. The Lie derivative and (given a connection) the covariant derivative extend to tensor fields of every type.
