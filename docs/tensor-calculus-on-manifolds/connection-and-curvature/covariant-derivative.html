The covariant derivative

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

The covariant derivative is the connection extended to all tensor fields, with a Christoffel correction added for every index. This page records the operational rules, the Levi-Civita formula, parallel transport, and geodesics.

Components

For a (r,s)(r, s)(r,s)-tensor field TTT, the covariant derivative ∇T\nabla T∇T is the (r,s+1)(r, s+1)(r,s+1)-tensor field with components

∇ρTμ1⋯μrν1⋯νs=∂ρTμ1⋯μrν1⋯νs+∑k=1rΓμkρλ Tμ1⋯λ⋯μrν1⋯νs−∑ℓ=1sΓλρνℓ Tμ1⋯μrν1⋯λ⋯νs.\nabla_\rho T^{\mu_1 \cdots \mu_r}{}_{\nu_1 \cdots \nu_s} = \partial_\rho T^{\mu_1 \cdots \mu_r}{}_{\nu_1 \cdots \nu_s} + \sum_{k=1}^{r} \Gamma^{\mu_k}{}_{\rho \lambda}\, T^{\mu_1 \cdots \lambda \cdots \mu_r}{}_{\nu_1 \cdots \nu_s} - \sum_{\ell=1}^{s} \Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\rho \nu_\ell}\, T^{\mu_1 \cdots \mu_r}{}_{\nu_1 \cdots \lambda \cdots \nu_s}. ∇ρ​Tμ1​⋯μr​ν1​⋯νs​​=∂ρ​Tμ1​⋯μr​ν1​⋯νs​​+k=1∑r​Γμk​ρλ​Tμ1​⋯λ⋯μr​ν1​⋯νs​​−ℓ=1∑s​Γλρνℓ​​Tμ1​⋯μr​ν1​⋯λ⋯νs​​.

One +Γ+\Gamma+Γ correction for every upper index; one −Γ-\Gamma−Γ correction for every lower index. Often written in the equivalent semicolon notation Tμ⋯ν⋯ ;ρ:=∇ρTμ⋯ν⋯T^{\mu \cdots}{}_{\nu \cdots ; \rho} := \nabla_\rho T^{\mu \cdots}{}_{\nu \cdots}Tμ⋯ν⋯;ρ​:=∇ρ​Tμ⋯ν⋯​.

Metric compatibility

A connection is metric-compatible if

∇ρgμν=0.\nabla_\rho g_{\mu\nu} = 0. ∇ρ​gμν​=0.

Equivalently ∇g=0\nabla g = 0∇g=0 as a tensor equation. Equivalently, the inner product is preserved by parallel transport: X(g(Y,Z))=g(∇XY,Z)+g(Y,∇XZ)X(g(Y, Z)) = g(\nabla_X Y, Z) + g(Y, \nabla_X Z)X(g(Y,Z))=g(∇X​Y,Z)+g(Y,∇X​Z) — a Leibniz rule with the metric playing the role of the multiplication.

Torsion-free

A connection is torsion-free (or symmetric) if its Christoffel symbols are symmetric in their lower indices:

Γρμν=Γρνμ.\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\mu}. Γρμν​=Γρνμ​.

Equivalently, ∇XY−∇YX=[X,Y]\nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X = [X, Y]∇X​Y−∇Y​X=[X,Y]. The next page treats torsion as a tensor in its own right; for now, this is the second of the two conditions that pin down a unique connection from a metric.

The Levi-Civita connection

Theorem (Fundamental theorem of pseudo-Riemannian geometry). On any pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M,g)(M, g)(M,g), there is a unique torsion-free metric-compatible connection. Its Christoffel symbols are given by the Christoffel formula:

Γρμν=12gρσ(∂μgνσ+∂νgσμ−∂σgμν).\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{1}{2} g^{\rho\sigma} \left( \partial_\mu g_{\nu\sigma} + \partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu} \right). Γρμν​=21​gρσ(∂μ​gνσ​+∂ν​gσμ​−∂σ​gμν​).

This is the Levi-Civita connection, and is the connection assumed throughout standard GR. The derivation is direct: write down the three permutations of ∇ρgμν=0\nabla_\rho g_{\mu\nu} = 0∇ρ​gμν​=0, take a signed sum, and use Γρμν=Γρνμ\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\mu}Γρμν​=Γρνμ​ to solve for Γ\GammaΓ.

Two consequences worth knowing:

  • Γμμν=∂νln⁡∣det⁡g∣\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu \ln \sqrt{|\det g|}Γμμν​=∂ν​ln∣detg∣​.
  • The covariant divergence simplifies: ∇μvμ=1∣det⁡g∣ ∂μ ⁣(∣det⁡g∣ vμ)\nabla_\mu v^\mu = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{|\det g|}}\, \partial_\mu \! \left( \sqrt{|\det g|}\, v^\mu \right)∇μ​vμ=∣detg∣​1​∂μ​(∣detg∣​vμ).

The second is the curved-space generalization of the divergence in vector calculus and lets you write conservation laws in a chart-independent form.

Geodesics

A curve γ\gammaγ is a geodesic if its velocity is parallel-transported along itself:

∇γ˙γ˙=0.\nabla_{\dot\gamma} \dot\gamma = 0. ∇γ˙​​γ˙​=0.

In coordinates,

For the Levi-Civita connection, geodesics are the locally length-extremizing curves — straightest possible paths and locally-shortest (Riemannian) or locally-stationary (Lorentzian) curves between their endpoints.

In Lorentzian signature, geodesics inherit a causal classification from their initial velocity: a curve with timelike γ˙\dot\gammaγ˙​ stays timelike (since metric-compatibility preserves g(γ˙,γ˙)g(\dot\gamma, \dot\gamma)g(γ˙​,γ˙​) along γ\gammaγ). Timelike geodesics are the worldlines of free-falling massive particles in GR; null geodesics, the worldlines of light.

The geodesic equation also follows from a variational principle: extremizing ∫gμν γ˙μγ˙ν dλ\int g_{\mu\nu}\, \dot\gamma^\mu \dot\gamma^\nu\, d\lambda∫gμν​γ˙​μγ˙​νdλ (the action) gives the geodesic equation as the Euler–Lagrange equation. The Christoffels appear as the price you pay for working in a non-trivial chart.

This variational form is the bridge to Noether’s theorem: a continuous symmetry of the geodesic action yields a conserved quantity along geodesics. The symmetries of gμν γ˙μγ˙νg_{\mu\nu}\, \dot\gamma^\mu \dot\gamma^\nugμν​γ˙​μγ˙​ν are exactly the isometries of ggg, generated by KKK, and the corresponding Noether charge is Kμγ˙μK_\mu \dot\gamma^\muKμ​γ˙​μ — conserved on every geodesic. The energy and angular momentum of orbits in Schwarzschild are the two instances of this.

Compact support

A function (or vector field, or differential form) has compact support if it vanishes outside some compact subset K⊆MK \subseteq MK⊆M. On a non-compact manifold this is the condition that makes ∫M\int_M∫M​ converge and that kills the boundary contributions at infinity — it is why Stokes’s theorem and the integration of nnn-forms are stated for compactly supported forms. On a compact manifold every smooth function has compact support automatically, so the qualifier can be dropped.

Minkowski space

The spacetime of special relativity: R4\mathbb{R}^4R4 with the global flat Lorentzian metric

η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2\eta = -dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2 η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2

(signature (−,+,+,+)(-, +, +, +)(−,+,+,+)). All Christoffel symbols vanish in these coordinates, so are straight lines and the Riemann tensor is zero — the “no gravity” special case that curved solutions approach asymptotically (Schwarzschild as r→∞r \to \inftyr→∞). Its group is the Poincaré group: translations plus Lorentz transformations. Every tangent space of a Lorentzian manifold is a copy of Minkowski space in miniature — the content of the Part I aside.

Embedded manifold

A smooth submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN is a subset M⊆RNM \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NM⊆RN that locally looks like the graph of a smooth function: around every p∈Mp \in Mp∈M there is an open U⊆RNU \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NU⊆RN, an open V⊆RnV \subseteq \mathbb{R}^nV⊆Rn, and a smooth embedding Φ:V→U∩M\Phi: V \to U \cap MΦ:V→U∩M whose differential is everywhere injective. The image Φ(V)\Phi(V)Φ(V) is an open piece of MMM, and the nnn coordinates on VVV are a chart on MMM.

The embedded view of differential geometry takes advantage of the ambient RN\mathbb{R}^NRN: tangent vectors are vectors in RN\mathbb{R}^NRN (sitting in the affine tangent plane to MMM); the metric is the pullback of the Euclidean inner product; integration is over an embedded submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN. The Whitney embedding theorem says every smooth nnn-manifold can be embedded in R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n, so the embedded view loses no generality in principle. In practice it can be inconvenient — Lorentzian spacetimes do not embed isometrically in any flat ambient — which is why the abstract view is also needed.
