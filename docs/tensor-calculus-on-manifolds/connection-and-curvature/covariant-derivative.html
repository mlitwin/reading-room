Covariant derivative, transport, geodesics

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

The connection extends to all tensor fields, with a Christoffel correction for every index. This page records the rules, then parallel transport and geodesics.

Extension to all tensors

Once ∇\nabla∇ is defined on vector fields, it extends uniquely to all tensor fields by the rules:

Crucially, parallel transport depends on the path, not just the endpoints — this path dependence is the curvature, made tensorial on page 4.

Geodesics

A curve γ\gammaγ is a geodesic if its velocity is parallel-transported along itself:

∇γ˙γ˙=0.\nabla_{\dot\gamma} \dot\gamma = 0. ∇γ˙​​γ˙​=0.

In coordinates,

γ¨ρ+Γρμν γ˙μγ˙ν=0,\ddot\gamma^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\, \dot\gamma^\mu \dot\gamma^\nu = 0, γ¨​ρ+Γρμν​γ˙​μγ˙​ν=0,

the geodesic equation. This is a system of nnn second-order ODEs; given γ(0)\gamma(0)γ(0) and γ˙(0)\dot\gamma(0)γ˙​(0), a unique geodesic exists on some interval around 000.

For the Levi-Civita connection, geodesics are the critical curves of length: locally shortest in Riemannian signature; in Lorentzian signature, timelike geodesics locally maximize proper time.

In Lorentzian signature, geodesics inherit a causal classification from their initial velocity: a geodesic with timelike initial velocity stays timelike, since metric compatibility makes g(γ˙,γ˙)\tens{g}(\dot\gamma, \dot\gamma)g(γ˙​,γ˙​) constant along it. Timelike geodesics are the worldlines of free-falling massive particles in GR; null geodesics, the worldlines of light.

The geodesic equation is also the of the action ∫12 gμν γ˙μγ˙ν dλ\int \tfrac12\, g_{\mu\nu}\, \dot\gamma^\mu \dot\gamma^\nu\, d\lambda∫21​gμν​γ˙​μγ˙​νdλ (chapter 10 does the computation). The Christoffels come from differentiating the position-dependent gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​ in the Euler–Lagrange equations.

Symmetries of the geodesic action give conserved quantities along geodesics; the next page derives them from Killing vectors.
