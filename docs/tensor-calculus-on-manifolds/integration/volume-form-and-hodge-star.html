Hodge star

A metric and orientation define the Hodge star ⋆:Ωk(M)→Ωn−k(M)\star: \Omega^k(M) \to \Omega^{n-k}(M)⋆:Ωk(M)→Ωn−k(M),

Orientation

An orientation of an nnn-manifold is a nowhere-vanishing nnn-form, taken up to multiplication by a positive function. A chart is positively oriented when dx1∧⋯∧dxndx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^ndx1∧⋯∧dxn agrees with it. An orientation is what makes ∫Mω\int_M \tens{\omega}∫M​ω of an nnn-form well-defined: the change-of-variables Jacobian then always has positive sign. The Möbius strip and Klein bottle admit none. volg=∣det⁡g∣  dx1∧⋯∧dxn\mathrm{vol}_g = \sqrt{|\det g|}\; dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n volg​=∣detg∣​dx1∧⋯∧dxn

Tensor density

A tensor density of weight www is a component array that transforms by the times an extra factor [det⁡(∂x′/∂x)]w\bigl[\det(\partial x'/\partial x)\bigr]^w[det(∂x′/∂x)]w of the Jacobian determinant. Weight 000 is an ordinary tensor. The symbol (lower indices) has weight +1+1+1; ∣det⁡g∣\sqrt{|\det g|}∣detg∣​ is a scalar density of weight −1-1−1; their product is an honest tensor — the general pattern, since multiplying by the appropriate power of ∣det⁡g∣\sqrt{|\det g|}∣detg∣​ converts any density to a tensor. Densities appear wherever determinants do: volume elements, integrands, and the ∣det⁡g∣\sqrt{|\det g|}∣detg∣​ factors of the variational formulas in chapter 15. (Sign conventions for www vary across textbooks — some count powers of det⁡(∂x/∂x′)\det(\partial x/\partial x')det(∂x/∂x′) instead.)

Tensor transformation law

Under a change of coordinates xμ↦x′μ′(x)x^\mu \mapsto x'^{\mu'}(x)xμ↦x′μ′(x), the components of an (r,s)(r, s)(r,s)-tensor transform as

T′μ1′⋯μr′ν1′⋯νs′=∂x′μ1′∂xμ1⋯∂x′μr′∂xμr  ∂xν1∂x′ν1′⋯∂xνs∂x′νs′  Tμ1⋯μrν1⋯νsT'^{\mu'_1 \cdots \mu'_r}{}_{\nu'_1 \cdots \nu'_s} = \frac{\partial x'^{\mu'_1}}{\partial x^{\mu_1}} \cdots \frac{\partial x'^{\mu'_r}}{\partial x^{\mu_r}}\; \frac{\partial x^{\nu_1}}{\partial x'^{\nu'_1}} \cdots \frac{\partial x^{\nu_s}}{\partial x'^{\nu'_s}}\; T^{\mu_1 \cdots \mu_r}{}_{\nu_1 \cdots \nu_s} T′μ1′​⋯μr′​ν1′​⋯νs′​​=∂xμ1​∂x′μ1′​​⋯∂xμr​∂x′μr′​​∂x′ν1′​∂xν1​​⋯∂x′νs′​∂xνs​​Tμ1​⋯μr​ν1​⋯νs​​

— one forward Jacobian factor per upper index, one inverse Jacobian factor per lower index. A component array obeying this rule on every chart overlap is a tensor; an array that picks up extra inhomogeneous terms (the Christoffel symbols are the standard example) is not.
