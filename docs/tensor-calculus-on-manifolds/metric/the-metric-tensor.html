A metric on MMM is a smooth (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2)- g\tens{g}g that is, at every point ppp:

At a single point, gp\tens{g}_pgp​ is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on one vector space, TpMT_p MTp​M. Everything on this page except curve length, pullback and existence is linear algebra at that one point.

Signature

By Sylvester’s law of inertia, TpMT_p MTp​M has a basis in which [gμν][g_{\mu\nu}][gμν​] is diagonal with entries ±1\pm 1±1. The number of each sign, the signature (n+,n−)(n_+, n_-)(n+​,n−​) with n++n−=nn_+ + n_- = nn+​+n−​=n, does not depend on the basis. Non-degeneracy keeps eigenvalues from crossing zero, so the signature is constant on a connected manifold. Three cases come up:

  • Riemannian, signature (n,0)(n, 0)(n,0): g\tens{g}g is positive-definite, an ordinary inner product on each tangent space.
  • Lorentzian, one negative sign. This book uses (−,+,+,+)(-, +, +, +)(−,+,+,+), as do MTW, Wald and Carroll; Landau–Lifshitz and particle physics use (+,−,−,−)(+, -, -, -)(+,−,−,−).
  • Pseudo-Riemannian: any signature, covering both.

Causal structure

A Lorentzian metric sorts the tangent vectors at each point into three classes:

  • timelike if g(v,v)<0\tens{g}(\tens{v}, \tens{v}) < 0g(v,v)<0: inside the lightcone;
  • null (lightlike) if g(v,v)=0\tens{g}(\tens{v}, \tens{v}) = 0g(v,v)=0, v≠0\tens{v} \neq 0v=0: on the lightcone;
  • spacelike if g(v,v)>0\tens{g}(\tens{v}, \tens{v}) > 0g(v,v)>0: outside it.

The null vectors form a double cone, the lightcone. A time orientation is a continuous choice of the future half of the lightcone at each point. Lorentzian manifolds that admit one are time-orientable; not all do.

In relativity, TpMT_p MTp​M with a Lorentzian gp\tens{g}_pgp​ is the local inertial frame at the event ppp. Four-velocities of massive particles are timelike and light rays are null. Everything special relativity says about one observer at one event is linear algebra in this tangent space. Gravity enters only through the variation of gp\tens{g}_pgp​ with ppp, which the connection and curvature measure.

Existence

Every manifold admits a Riemannian metric: glue local Euclidean metrics with a . Lorentzian metrics are more restrictive. A closed (compact, boundaryless) manifold admits one iff it has a nowhere-vanishing vector field, equivalently iff its vanishes. Among closed surfaces only the torus and the Klein bottle qualify; S2S^2S2, with χ=2\chi = 2χ=2, does not. GR is set on non-compact spacetimes for a causal reason: every compact Lorentzian manifold contains a closed timelike curve.

Minkowski space

The spacetime of special relativity: R4\mathbb{R}^4R4 with the global flat Lorentzian metric

η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2\tens{\eta} = -dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2 η=−dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2

(signature (−,+,+,+)(-, +, +, +)(−,+,+,+)). All Christoffel symbols vanish in these coordinates, so are straight lines and the Riemann tensor is zero — the “no gravity” special case that curved solutions approach asymptotically (Schwarzschild as r→∞r \to \inftyr→∞). Its group is the Poincaré group: translations plus Lorentz transformations. Every tangent space of a Lorentzian manifold is a copy of Minkowski space in miniature (causal structure).

Embedded manifold

A smooth submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN is a subset M⊆RNM \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NM⊆RN that is smoothly parametrized near each point: around every p∈Mp \in Mp∈M there is an open U⊆RNU \subseteq \mathbb{R}^NU⊆RN, an open V⊆RnV \subseteq \mathbb{R}^nV⊆Rn, and a smooth map Φ:V→RN\Phi: V \to \mathbb{R}^NΦ:V→RN with everywhere-injective differential that is a homeomorphism onto U∩MU \cap MU∩M. The image Φ(V)\Phi(V)Φ(V) is an open piece of MMM, and the nnn coordinates on VVV are a chart on MMM.

The embedded view of differential geometry takes advantage of the ambient RN\mathbb{R}^NRN: tangent vectors are vectors in RN\mathbb{R}^NRN (spanning the tangent plane to MMM); the metric is the pullback of the Euclidean inner product; integration is over an embedded submanifold of RN\mathbb{R}^NRN. The Whitney embedding theorem says every smooth nnn-manifold can be embedded in R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n, so the embedded view loses no generality in principle. In practice the ambient space is excess baggage — isometric embeddings need many more dimensions (Nash), and a Lorentzian metric can only be induced from an indefinite-signature ambient space — which is why the abstract view is the working one.

Second countable

A topological space is second countable if its topology has a countable base: countably many open sets such that every open set is a union of some of them. For a metric space this is equivalent to having a countable dense subset: balls of rational radius about its points form a countable base. This is what makes and arguments work on a manifold.

Hausdorff

A topological space is Hausdorff if any two distinct points p,qp, qp,q have disjoint open neighborhoods U∋pU \ni pU∋p, V∋qV \ni qV∋q with U∩V=∅U \cap V = \emptysetU∩V=∅. Most spaces appearing in differential geometry are Hausdorff: Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, every metric space, every — and every manifold, because the definition of a manifold demands it. The non-Hausdorff cases are exotic — the “line with two origins” is the canonical example.

Paracompact

A topological space is paracompact if every open cover admits a locally finite open refinement — a cover by smaller sets, each inside some original set, such that every point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many of them. For manifolds the property comes for free: plus implies paracompact, and paracompactness is exactly what a needs to exist. It is the standard topological hypothesis under which the local-to-global constructions of differential geometry (gluing metrics, defining integration) go through.

Compact support

A function (or vector field, or differential form) has compact support if it vanishes outside some compact subset K⊆MK \subseteq MK⊆M. On a non-compact manifold this is the condition that makes ∫M\int_M∫M​ converge and that kills the boundary contributions at infinity — it is why Stokes’s theorem and the integration of nnn-forms are stated for compactly supported forms. On a compact manifold every smooth function has compact support automatically, so the qualifier can be dropped.
