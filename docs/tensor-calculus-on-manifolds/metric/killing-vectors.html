Killing vectors

Matthew Litwin 2026-06-23

The symmetries of a metric, made infinitesimal. This page uses the Lie derivative from Part I and quotes one formula from chapter 9.

Isometries

A diffeomorphism F:M→MF: M \to MF:M→M with

F∗g=gF^* g = g F∗g=g

is an isometry — it preserves every length and angle the metric defines. The isometries of (M,g)(M, g)(M,g) form a group. For the round sphere it is O(3)O(3)O(3), with connected part SO(3)SO(3)SO(3); for Minkowski space, the Poincaré group.

Killing fields

A vector field K∈X(M)K \in \mathfrak{X}(M)K∈X(M) is a Killing vector field if its flow preserves the metric:

LKg=0.\mathcal{L}_K g = 0. LK​g=0.

The flow of a Killing field is a one-parameter family of isometries — a continuous symmetry of the geometry. For the Levi-Civita connection this is equivalent to the Killing equation

∇μKν+∇νKμ=0\nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu = 0 ∇μ​Kν​+∇ν​Kμ​=0

(with ∇\nabla∇ the covariant derivative of chapter 9; ∇K\nabla K∇K is antisymmetric).

Coordinate shortcut. If every component gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​ is independent of some coordinate xkx^kxk, then ∂k\partial_k∂k​ is Killing — “the metric doesn’t change along xkx^kxk.” Most Killing fields met in practice are found this way.

Killing fields are closed under the Lie bracket, so they form a — the Lie algebra of the isometry group. Its dimension is at most n(n+1)/2n(n+1)/2n(n+1)/2; spaces achieving the bound are maximally symmetric (spheres, Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces, de Sitter).

On the sphere

The round metric g=dθ2+sin⁡2θ dφ2g = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\varphi^2g=dθ2+sin2θdφ2 has no φ\varphiφ-dependence, so ∂φ\partial_\varphi∂φ​ is Killing — rotation about the ZZZ-axis. The full Killing algebra is three-dimensional, so(3)\mathfrak{so}(3)so(3), one field per rotation axis:

Three is the maximum 12n(n+1)=3\tfrac{1}{2}n(n+1) = 321​n(n+1)=3 for n=2n = 2n=2: the round sphere is maximally symmetric. Only KZK_ZKZ​ is visible as a coordinate symmetry of the standard chart; the other two mix θ\thetaθ and φ\varphiφ, which is the usual situation — a chart adapts to at most a few of the symmetries at once.

Toward Schwarzschild

The Schwarzschild metric of chapter 10 is independent of both ttt and φ\varphiφ, so ∂t\partial_t∂t​ and ∂φ\partial_\varphi∂φ​ are Killing. Their conserved pairings are the energy EEE and angular momentum LLL that reduce the orbit calculation to a one-dimensional problem — the entire tractability of Schwarzschild geodesics is Killing symmetry at work.

Lie algebra

A Lie algebra over R\mathbb{R}R is a real vector space g\mathfrak{g}g equipped with a bilinear antisymmetric bracket [⋅,⋅]:g×g→g[\cdot, \cdot]: \mathfrak{g} \times \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}[⋅,⋅]:g×g→g satisfying the Jacobi identity

[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0.[X, [Y, Z]] + [Y, [Z, X]] + [Z, [X, Y]] = 0. [X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0.

Examples: gln(R)\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R})gln​(R) — n×nn \times nn×n real matrices with [A,B]=AB−BA[A, B] = AB - BA[A,B]=AB−BA; the tangent space at the identity of any Lie group; the vector fields on a manifold under the Lie bracket. The vector-fields Lie algebra X(M)\mathfrak{X}(M)X(M) is infinite-dimensional and not the Lie algebra of any finite-dimensional Lie group.
