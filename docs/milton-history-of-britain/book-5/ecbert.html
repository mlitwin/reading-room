John Milton 2026-11-19

Ecbert.

in full highth of glory, having now enjoy’d his Conquest seaven peacefull years, his victorious Army long since disbanded, and the exercise of Armes perhaps laid aside, the more was found unprovided against a sudden storm of Danes from the Sea, who landing in . of his Reign, wasted in Kent. Ecbert the next year, gathering an Army, for he had heard of in 35 Ships, gave them Battail Carr in Dorsetshire; the event wherof was, that the Danes kept thir ground, and encampt where the field was fought; two Saxon Leaders, Dudda and Osmund, and two Bishops, as , were there slain. This was the only check of Fortune we read of, that Ecbert in all his time receav’d. For the Danes returning with a great Navy, and with the Cornish, who had enterd League with them, were overthrown and put to flight. Of these invasions against Ecbert, the Danish History is ; whether out of thir own Records or ours, may be justly doubted: for of these times at home, I find them in much uncertainty, and beholding rather to Out-landish Chronicles, then any Records of thir own. The Victor Ecbert, as one who had done enough, seasonably now, after prosperous success, the next ended his days, and at Winchester.
