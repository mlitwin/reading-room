Milton: The History of Britain

John Milton 2026-11-19 miltonhistoryproseearly-modernbritain

Milton began The History of Britain in 1649, in the weeks after the execution of Charles I. He meant to carry the story from the earliest legends down to his own day, and he got as far as the Norman Conquest. It was printed in 1670 and again in 1671. He wrote it “with plain, and lightsom brevity, to relate well and orderly things worth the noting”: it is a republican’s history, sceptical of fables and severe on bad kings and worse clergy.

This is the 1671 text as edited by Thomas H. Luxon. Underlined phrases open the editor’s annotations, which cover Milton’s marginal sources, people, places and dates. Words with a faint dotted underline are old spellings: hover over one, or tap it, to see the modern form. Italic and roman type follow the 1671 printing.

Contents

  1. Introduction: Luxon on the composition, the censored Digression, and the choice of text.
  2. Book I: the legendary kings, from Brutus to Cassibelan.
  3. Book II: the Romans in Britain.
  4. Book III: from the Roman withdrawal through the Saxon conquest.
  5. The Digression (Manuscript): the comparison of the post-Roman Britons with the Long Parliament. It was left out of the first edition, and the manuscript places it in Book III after the words “from one misery to another”.
  6. Book IV: the Saxon kingdoms, from Gregory’s mission to the English to Ecbert.
  7. Book V: the West-Saxon kings and the Danes, by reign, from Ecbert to Edgar.
  8. Book VI: from Edward the Younger to Harold and the Conquest, by reign.
  9. The Character of the Long Parliament (1681): Roger L’Estrange’s royalist printing of the Digression, given here as a separate document.
