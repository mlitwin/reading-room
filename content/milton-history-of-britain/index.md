Milton began *The History of Britain* in 1649, in the weeks after the execution of Charles I. He meant to carry the story from the earliest legends down to his own day, and he got as far as the Norman Conquest. It was printed in 1670 and again in 1671. He wrote it "with plain, and lightsom brevity, to relate well and orderly things worth the noting": it is a republican's history, sceptical of fables and severe on bad kings and worse clergy.

This is the 1671 text as edited by Thomas H. Luxon. Underlined phrases open the editor's annotations, which cover Milton's marginal sources, people, places and dates. Words with a faint dotted underline are old spellings: hover over one, or tap it, to see the modern form. Italic and roman type follow the 1671 printing.
