## The Fourth Book.

</div>

THE <i>Saxons</i> grown up now to [7 absolute Kingdoms](note:7kingdoms), and the latest of them <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="established">establish'd</span> by succession, finding <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> power arrive well nigh at the utmost of what was to be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="gained">gain'd</span> upon the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Britons">Britans</span></i>, and as little fearing to be displanted by them, had time now to survey at <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="leisure">leasure</span> one <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="another&#x27;s">anothers</span> greatness. Which quickly bred among them, either envy or mutual jealousies; till the [West Kingdom](note:westkingdom) at length grown over powerful, put an end to all the rest. Meanwhile, [above others](note:alltherest), <i>Ethelbert</i> of <i>Kent</i>, who by this time had well <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="ripened">rip'nd</span> his young ambition, with more ability of years and experience in War, what before he attempted to his loss, now successfully attains; and by degrees brought all the other Monarchies between <i>Kent</i> and <i>Humber</i> to be at his devotion. To which design the Kingdom of <i>West Saxons</i>, being the firmest of them all, at that time sore <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="shaken">shak'n</span> by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> overthrow at <i>Wodens-beorth</i>, and the Death of <i>Keaulin</i>, gave him no doubt a main advantage; the rest <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="yielded">yeilded</span> not subjection, but as he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="earned">earn'd</span> it by [continual Victories](note:continualvictories). And to win him the more regard abroad, he marries <i>Bertha</i> the <i>French</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="King&#x27;s">Kings</span> Daughter, though a Christian, and with this condition, to have the free exercise of her Faith, under the care and instruction of <i>Letardus</i> a Bishop, sent by her Parents along with her; the King notwithstanding and his people retaining <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own Religion. [<i>Beda</i>](note:beda) out of <i>Gildas</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="lays">laies</span> it sadly to the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Britons&#x27;">Britans</span></i> charge, that they never would vouchsafe <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> <i>Saxon</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Neighbors">Neighbours</span> the means of conversion: but how far to blame they were, and what hope there was of [converting](note:converting) in the midst of so much hostility, at least <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="falsehood">falshood</span> from <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> first arrival, is not now <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="easy">easie</span> to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="determine">determin</span>. Howbeit not long after, they had the Christian Faith <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="preached">preach't</span> to them by a [Nation more remote](note:anation), and (as a report went, accounted old in <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Beda&#x27;s">Bedas</span></i> time) upon this occasion.

The <i>Northumbrians</i> had a custom at that time, and many <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="hundred">hunder'd</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="years">yeares</span> after not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="abolished">abolish't</span>, to sell <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Children">Childern</span> for a small value into any <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Foreign">Foren</span> Land. Of which number two <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="comely">comly</span> youths were brought to <i>Rome</i>, whose fair and honest <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="countenances">countnances</span> invited [<i>Gregory</i>](note:gregorygreat) Arch-Deacon of that <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="City">Citty</span>, among others that beheld them, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="pitying">pittying</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> condition, to demand whence they were; it was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="answered">answer'd</span> by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="some">som</span> who stood by, that they were <i>Angli</i> of the Province <i>Deira</i>, subjects to <i>Alla</i> King of <i>Northumberland</i>, and by Religion, Pagans. Which last <i>Gregory</i> deploring, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="framed">fram'd</span> on a sudden this allusion to the three names he heard; that the <i>Angli</i> so like to Angels should be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="snatched">snatch't</span> <i>de ira</i>, that is, from the wrath of God, to sing <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Halleluia">Hallelujah</span></i>: and forthwith obtaining licence of <i>Benedict</i> the Pope, had come and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="preached">preach't</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="here">heer</span> among them, had not the <i>Roman</i> people, whose love <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="endured">endur'd</span> not the absence of so vigilant a Pastor over them, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="recalled">recall'd</span> him then on his journey, though. For a while after, succeeding in the Papal Seat, and now in his fourth year, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="admonished">admonisht</span>, saith <i>Beda</i>, by divine instinct, he sent <i>[Augustine](note:piousintention)</i> whom he had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="designed">design'd</span> for Bishop of the <i>English</i> Nation, and other zealous Monks with him, to preach to them the Gospel. Who being now on <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> way, discouraged by some reports, or <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own carnal fear, sent back <i>Austin</i>, in the name of all, to beseech <i>Gregory</i> they might return home, and not be sent a journey so full of hazard, to a fierce and infidel Nation, whose tongue they understood not. <i>Gregory</i> with pious and Apostolic <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="persuasions">perswasions</span> exhorts them not to shrink back from so good a work, but cheerfully to go on in the strength of divine assistance. The [Letter it self](note:letteritself) yet extant among our Writers of Ecclesiastic story, I omit <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="here">heer</span>, as not professing to relate of those matters more <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> what mixes aptly with civil affairs. The Abbott <i>Austin</i>, for so he was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="ordained">ordain'd</span> over the rest, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reencouraged">reincourag'd</span> by the exhortations of <i>Gregory</i>, and his fellows by the Letter which he brought them, [came safe](note:camesafe) to the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Isle">Ile</span> of <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Thanet">Tanet</span></i>, in number about 40, besides some of the French Nation whom they took along as Interpreters. <i>Ethelbert</i> the King, to whom <i>Austin</i> at his landing had sent a new and wondrous message, that he came from <i>Rome</i> to proffer <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Heaven">Heav'n</span> and eternal happiness in the knowledge of another God <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> the <i>Saxons</i> knew, appoints them to remain where they landed, and necessaries to be provided them, consulting in the mean time what was to be done. And after certain days coming into the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Island">Iland</span>, chose a place to meet them under the open Sky, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="possessed">possest</span> with an old <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="persuasion">perswasion</span>, that all Spells, if they should use any to deceive him, so it were not within doors, would be unavailable. They on the other side <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="called">call'd</span> to his presence, advancing for <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> Standard, a silver cross, and the painted image of our <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Savior">Saviour</span>, came slowly forward singing <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> solemn Litanies: which wrought in <i>Ethelbert</i> more <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="suspicion">suspition</span> perhaps that they <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="used">us'd</span> [enchantments](note:enchantments); till sitting down as the King <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wiilled">will'd</span> them, they there <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="preached">preach'd</span> to him and all in that assembly, the tidings of Salvation. Whom having heard attentively, the King thus <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="answered">answer'd</span>. Fair indeed and ample are the promises which ye bring, and such things as have the appearance in them of much good; yet such as being new and uncertain, I cannot hastily assent to, quitting the Religion which from my Ancestors, with all the <i>English</i> Nation, so many years I have <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="retained">retain'd</span>. Nevertheless because ye are Strangers, and have <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="endured">endur'd</span> so long a journey, to impart us the knowledge of things, which I <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="persuade">perswade</span> me you believe to be the truest and the best, ye may be sure we shall not recompence you with any molestation, but shall provide rather how we may friendliest entertain ye; nor do we forbid whom ye can by preaching gain to your belief. And accordingly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> residence he allotted them in <i>[Doroverne](note:doroverne)</i> or <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Canterbury">Canturbury</span></i> his chief City, and made provision for <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> maintenance, with free leave to preach their doctrine where they pleased. By which, and by the example of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> holy life, spent in prayer, fasting, and continual <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="labor">labour</span> in the conversion of Souls, they won many; on whose bounty and the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="King&#x27;s">Kings</span>, receiving only what was necessary, they subsisted. There stood without the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="City">Citty</span>, on the East-side, an ancient Church built in <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="honor">honour</span> of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Saint">St.</span> <i>Martin</i>, while yet the <i>Romans</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="remained">remain'd</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="here">heer</span>: in which <i>Bertha</i> the Queen went out usually to pray: <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Here">Heer</span> they also began first to preach, baptize, and openly to exercise [divine worship](note:divineworship). But when the King himself, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="convinced">convinc't</span> by their good life &amp; miracles, became Christian, and was baptized, which came to pass in the very first year of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> arrival, then multitudes daily, conforming to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> Prince, thought it <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="honor">honour</span> to be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reckoned">reckon'd</span> among those of his faith. To whom <i>Ethelbert</i> indeed principally <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="showed">shewed</span> his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="favor">favour</span>, but [<span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="compelled">compell'd</span> none](note:compelldnone). For so he had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="been">bin</span> taught by them who were both the Instructors and the Authors of his faith, that Christian Religion ought to be voluntary, not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="compelled">compell'd</span>. About this time <i>Kelwulf</i> the Son of <i>Cutha</i> <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Keaulin&#x27;s">Keaulins</span></i> Brother <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reigned">reign'd</span> over the <i>West-Saxons</i>, after his [Brother <i>Keola</i>](note:brotherkeola) or <i>Kelric</i>, and had continual War either with <i>English</i>, <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Welsh">Welch</span></i>, <i>Picts</i>, or <i>Scots</i>. But [<i>Austin</i>](note:austin), whom with his fellows, <i>Ethelbert</i> now had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="endowed">endow'd</span> with a better place for <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> abode in the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="City">Citty</span>, and other possessions necessary to livelihood, crossing into <i>France</i>, was by the Archbishop of <i>Arles</i>, at the appointment of Pope <i>Gregory</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="ordained">ordain'd</span> [Archbishop](note:endowd) of the <i>English</i>: and returning, sent to <i>Rome</i> <i>Laurence</i> and <i>Peter</i>, two of his associates, to acquaint the Pope of his good success in <i>England</i>, and to be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="resolved">resolv'd</span> of certain Theological, or rather [Levitical](note:levitical) questions: with answers to which, not proper in this place, <i>Gregory</i> sends also to the great work of converting, that went on so happily, a supply of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="laborers">[labourers](note:labourers)</span>, [<i>Mellitus</i>](note:mellitus), <i>Justus</i>, <i>Paulinus</i>, <i>Rufinian</i>, and many others; who what they were, may be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="guessed">guess't</span> by the stuff which they brought with them, vessels and vestments for the Altar, [<span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Copes">Coaps</span>](note:coaps), <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="relics">reliques</span>, and for the Archbishop <i>Austin</i> a Pall to say Mass in: to such a rank superstition that Age was grown, though some of them yet retaining an emulation of Apostolic zeal: lastly, to <i>Ethelbert</i> they brought a letter with many [presents](note:manypresents). <i>Austin</i> thus exalted to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Archepiscopal">Archiepiscopal</span> authority, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="recovered">recover'd</span> from the ruins and other prophane uses, a Christian Church in <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Canterbury">Canturbury</span></i> built of old by the <i>Romans</i>, which he dedicated by the name of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Christ&#x27;s">Christs</span> Church, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="joining">joyning</span> to it built a seat for himself and his successors; a [Monastery](note:stpeters) also <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="near">neer</span> the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="City">Citty</span> Eastward, where <i>Ethelbert</i> at his motion built <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Saint">St.</span> <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Peter&#x27;s">Peters</span></i>, and enrich't it with great endowments, to be a place of burial for the Archbishops and Kings of <i>Kent</i>: so quickly they <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="stepped">step't</span> up into fellowship of pomp with Kings. While thus <i>Ethelbert</i> and his people had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> minds intent, <i>Ethelfrid</i> the <i>Northumbrian</i> King was [not less busied](note:mindsintent) in far different affairs: for being altogether warlike, and covetous of fame, he more wasted the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Britons">Britans</span></i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> any <i>Saxon</i> King before him; winning from them large Territories, which either he made tributary, or planted with his own Subjects. Whence [<i>Edan</i>](note:edan) King of those <i>Scots</i> that dwelt in <i>Britain</i>, jealous of his successes, came against him with a mighty Army, to a place <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="called">call'd</span> [<i>Degsastan</i>](note:degsastan); but in the fight <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="losing">loosing</span> most of his men, himself with a few <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="escaped">escap'd</span>: only <i>Theobald</i> the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="King&#x27;s">Kings</span> brother, and the whole wing which he commanded, unfortunately cut off, made the Victory to <i>Ethelfrid</i> less <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="entire">intire</span>. Yet from that time no King of <i>Scots</i> in hostile manner durst pass into <i>Britain</i> for a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="hundred">hunderd</span> and more years after: and what some years before <i>Kelwulf</i> the <i>West-Saxon</i> is <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="annaled">annal'd</span> to have done against the <i>Scots</i> and <i>Picts</i>, passing through the Land of <i>Ethelfrid</i> a King so potent, unless in his aid and alliance, is not likely. <i>[Buchanan](note:buchwrites)</i> writes as if <i>Ethelfrid</i>, assisted by <i>Keaulin</i> whom he mistitles King of <i>East-Saxons</i>, had before this time a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="battle">battel</span> with <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Aedan">Aidan</span></i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wherein">wherin</span> <i>Cutha</i> <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Keaulin&#x27;s">Keaulins</span></i> son, was slain. But <i>Cutha</i>, as is above written from better authority, was slain in fight against the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Welsh">Welch</span></i> 20 years before. The number of Christians began now [to increase](note:toincrease) so fast, that <i>Augustine</i> [ordaining Bishops](note:ordainingbishops) under him, two of his assistants <i>Mellitus</i> and <i>Justus</i>, sent them out both to the work of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> ministry. And <i>Mellitus</i> by preaching converted the <i>East-Saxons</i>, over whom <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Saebert">Sebehrt</span></i> the son of <i>Sleda</i>, by permission of <i>Ethelbert</i>, being [born of his sister](note:bornofhissister) <i>Ricula</i>, then <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reigned">reign'd</span>. Whose conversion <i>Ethelbert</i> to gratulate, built them the great Church of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Saint">St.</span> <i>Paul</i> in <i>London</i> to be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Bishop&#x27;s">Bishops</span> Cathedral; as <i>Justus</i> also had his built at <i>Rochester</i>, and both gifted by the same King with fair possessions. Hitherto <i>Austin</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="labored">laboured</span> well among Infidels, but not with like commendation soon after among Christians. For by means of <i>Ethelbert</i> summoning the <i>Britain</i> Bishops to [a place on the edge](note:aplaceontheedge) of <i>Worcestershire</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="called">call'd</span> from that time <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Augustine&#x27;s">Augustines</span></i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Oak">Oke</span>, he requires them to conform with him in the same day of celebrating <i>Easter</i>, and many other points wherein they <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="differed">differ'd</span> from the rites of <i>Rome</i>: which when they <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="refused">refus'd</span> to do, not prevailing by dispute, he appeals to a miracle, restoring to sight a blind man whom the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Britons">Britans</span></i> could not cure. At this something <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="moved">mov'd</span>, though not minded to recede from <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own opinions without furder consultation, they request a second meeting: to which came seven <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Briton">Britan</span></i> Bishops, with many other <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="learned">lerned</span> men, especially from the famous [Monastery of <i>Bangor</i>](note:bangor), in which were said to be so many Monks, living all by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="labor">labour</span>, that being divided under seven Rectors, none had fewer <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> 300. One man there was who staid behind, a Hermit by the life he led, who by his wisdom effected more <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> all the rest who went: being demanded, for they held him as an Oracle, how they might know <i>Austin</i> to be a man from God, that they might follow him, he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="answered">answer'd</span>, that if they found him meek and humble, they should be taught by him, for it was likeliest to be the yoke of Christ, both what he bore himself, and would have them bear; but if he bore himself proudly, that they should not regard him, for he was then certainly not of God. They took his advice, and hasted to the place of meeting. Whom <i>Austin</i> being already there before them, neither arose to meet, nor <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="received">receiv'd</span> in any brotherly sort, but sat all the while pontifically in his Chair. Whereat the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Britons">Britans</span></i>, as they were <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="counseled">counsel'd</span> by the holy man, neglected him, and neither <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="hearkened">hark'n'd</span> to his proposals of conformity, nor would [acknowledge him](note:acknowledgehim) for an Archbishop: And in name of the rest, <i>Dinothus</i> then Abbot of <i>Bangor</i>, is said, thus sagely to have <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="answered">answer'd</span> him. As to the subjection which you require, be thus <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="persuaded">perswaded</span> of us, that in the bond of love and charity we are all Subjects and Servants to the Church of God, yea to the Pope of <i>Rome</i>, and every good Christian to help them forward, both by word and deed, to be the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Children">Childern</span> of God: other obedience <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> this we know not to be due to him whom you term the Pope; and this obedience we are ready to give both to him and to every Christian continually. Besides, we are <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="governed">govern'd</span> under God by the Bishop of [<i>Caerleon</i>](note:caerleon), who is to oversee us in spiritual matters. To which <i>Austin</i> thus presaging, [some say menacing](note:menacing), replies, since ye refuse to accept of peace with your brethren, ye shall have War from your enemies; and since ye will not with us preach the word of life, to whom ye ought, from their hands ye shall receive death. This, though Writers agree not whether <i>Austin</i> spake it as his prophecy, or as his plot against the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Britons">Britans</span></i>, fell out accordingly. For many years were [not past](note:notpast), when <i>Ethelfrid</i> whether of his own accord, or at [the request](note:therequest) of <i>Ethelbert</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="incensed">incens't</span> by <i>Austin</i>, with a powerful host came to <i>Westchester</i>, then <i>Caer-legion</i>.
