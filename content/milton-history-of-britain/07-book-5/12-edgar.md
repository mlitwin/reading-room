### <i>Edgar</i>.

</div>

[<i>EDgar</i>](note:kingedgar) by his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Brother&#x27;s">Brothers</span> Death, [now King](note:959) of all <i>England</i> at [16 years](note:16years) of Age, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="called">call'd</span> home <i>Dunstan</i> out of <i>Flanders</i>, where he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="lived">liv'd</span> in exile. This King had no War all his Reign; yet <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="always">allways</span> well <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="prepared">prepar'd</span> for War, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="governed">govern'd</span> the Kingdom in great Peace, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Honor">Honour</span>, and Prosperity, gaining thence the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Surname">Sirname</span> of Peaceable, much <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="extolled">extoll'd</span> for Justice, Clemency, and all Kingly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Virtues">Vertues</span>, the more, ye may be sure, [by Monks](note:bymonks), for his building so many Monasteries; as [some write](note:assomewritemalms), every year one: for he much <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="favored">favour'd</span> the Monks against secular <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Priests">Preists</span>, who in the time of <i>Edwi</i> had got possession in most of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Convents">Covents</span>. His care and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wisdom">wisdome</span> was great in guarding the Coast round with stout ships, to the number of 3600, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Matthew">Mat.</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Westminster">West.</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reckons">reck'ns</span> them [4800](note:matwest4800), divided into <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="four">fowr</span> Squadrons, to sail to and fro about the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="four">fowr</span> quarters of the land, meeting each other; the first of 1200 sail from East to West, the second of as many from West to East, the third and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="fourth">fowrth</span> between North and South; himself in the Summer time with his Fleet. Thus he kept out wisely the force of Strangers, and prevented <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Foreign">Forein</span> War; but by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> too frequent resort hither in time of peace, and his too much <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="favoring">favouring</span> them, he let in <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> vices unaware. Thence the people, saith <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Malmesbury">Malmsbury</span></i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="learned">learnt</span> of the [out-landish](note:outlandish) <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Saxons">Saxons</span></i> rudeness, of the <i>Flemish</i> daintiness and softness; of the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Danes">Danes</span></i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Drunkenness">Drunk'ness</span>; though I doubt these vices are as naturally homebred <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="here">heer</span> as in any of those Countries. Yet in the Winter and Spring time he usually rode the Circuit as a Judge Itinerant through all his Provinces, to see justice well <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="administered">administerd</span>, and the poor not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="oppressed">oppress'd</span>. <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Thieves">Theeves</span> and Robbers he rooted almost out of the Land, and wild Beasts of prey altogether; enjoining <i>Ludwal</i> King of <i>Wales</i> to pay the yearly tribute of 300 Wolves, which he did for two years together, till the third year no more were to be found, nor ever after; but his Laws may be read yet extant. Whatever was the cause, he was not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Crowned">Crown'd</span> till the [30. of his Age](note:973), but then with great splendor and [magnificence](note:magnificence) at the City of <i>Bath</i>, in the Feast of <i>Pentecost</i>. This year died <i>Swarling</i> a Monk of <i>Croyland</i>, in the 142. year of his Age, and another [soon after](note:974) him in [the 115<i>th</i>](note:115th). in that Fenny and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="waterish">watrish</span> air, the more remarkable. King <i>Edgar</i> the next year went to <i>Chester</i>, and summoning to his Court there [all the Kings](note:allthekings973) that held of him, took homage of them: <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> names are <i>Kened</i> King of <i>Scots</i>, <i>Malcolm</i> of <i>Cumberland</i>, <i>Maccuse</i> of the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Isles">Iles</span>, five of <i>Wales</i>, <i>Duswal</i>, <i>Huwal</i>, <i>Grifith</i>, <i>Jacob</i>, <i>Judethil</i>, these he had in such <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="awe">aw</span>, that going one day into a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Galley">Gally</span>, he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="caused">caus'd</span> them to take each man his Oar, and row him down the [River <i>Dee</i>](note:dee), while he himself sat at the Stern: which might be done in <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="merriment">meriment</span>, and easily <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="obeyed">obei'd</span>; if with a serious brow, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="discovered">discoverd</span> rather vain glory, and insulting haughtiness, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> moderation of mind. And that he did it seriously <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="triumphing">tryumphing</span>, appears by his words then <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="uttered">utterd</span>, that his successors might then glory to be Kings of <i>England</i>, when they had such <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="honor">honour</span> done them. And perhaps the Divine power was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="displeased">displeas'd</span> with him for taking too much <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="honor">honour</span> to himself; since we read that the [year following](note:975) he was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="taken">tak'n</span> out of this life by sickness in the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="height">highth</span> of his glory and the prime of his Age, buried at [<i>Glaston</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Abbey">Abby</span>](note:glastonabby). The same year, as <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Matthew">Mat.</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Westminster">West.</span></i> relates, he gave to <i>Kened</i> the <i>Scottish</i> King, many [rich presents](note:richpresents), and the whole Country of <i>Laudian</i>, or <i>Lothien</i>, to hold of him on condition that he and his successors should repair to the English Court at high Festivals when the King sat <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Crowned">Crown'd</span>; gave him also many lodging places by the way, which till the days of <i>Henry</i> the second were still held by the Kings of <i>Scotland</i>. He was of Stature not tall, of body slender, yet so well made, that in strength he chose to contend with such as were thought strongest, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="disliked">dislik'd</span> nothing more <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> that they should spare him for respect or fear to hurt him. <i>Kened</i> King of <i>Scots</i> then in the Court of <i>Edgar</i>, sitting [one day at Table](note:onedayattable) was heard to say jestingly among his Servants, he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wondered">wonderd</span> how so many Provinces could be held in subjection by such a little dapper man: his words were brought to the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="King&#x27;s">Kings</span> Ear; he sends for <i>Kened</i> as about some private business, and in talk drawing him forth to a secret place, takes from under his garment two Swords which he had brought with him, gave one of them to <i>Kened</i>; and now saith he, it shall be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="tried">try'd</span> which ought to be the subject; for it is <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="shameful">shamefull</span> for a King to boast at Table, and shrink in fight. <i>Kened</i> much <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="abashed">abash'd</span> fell presently at his Feet, and besought him to pardon what he had simply <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="spoken">spok'n</span>, no way intended to his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="dishonor">dishonour</span> or disparagment: wherewith the King was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="satisfied">satisfi'd</span>. <i>Camden</i> in his description of <i>Ireland</i>, [cites a Charter](note:citesacharter) of King <i>Edgar</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wherein">wherin</span> it appears, he had in subjection all the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Kingdoms">Kingdomes</span> of the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Isles">Iles</span> as far as <i>Norway</i>, and had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="subdued">subdu'd</span> the greatest part of <i>Ireland</i> with the City of <i>Dublin</i>: but of this other Writers make no mention. In his youth having heard of [<i>Elfrida</i>](note:elfrida), Daughter of <i>Ordgar</i> Duke of <i>Devonshire</i>, much commended for her Beauty, he sent [Earl <i>Athelwold</i>](note:athelwald), whose loyalty he trusted most, to see her; intending, if she were found such as <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="answered">answerd</span> report, to demand her in marriage. He at the first view <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="taken">tak'n</span> with her presence, disloyally, as it oft <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="happens">happ'ns</span> in such <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="employments">emploiments</span>, began to sue for himself; and with consent of her Parents <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="obtained">obtain'd</span> her. Returning <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="therefore">therfore</span> with scarce an ordinary commendation of her Feature, he easily took off the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="King&#x27;s">Kings</span> mind, soon diverted another way. But the matter coming to light how <i>Athelwold</i> had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="forestalled">forestall'd</span> the King, and <i>Elfrida</i>'s Beauty more and more <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="spoken">spok'n</span> of, the King now heated not only with a relapse of Love, but with a deep <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="sense">sence</span> of the abuse, yet dissembling his disturbance, pleasantly told the Earl, what day he meant to come and visit him and his fair Wife. The Earl seemingly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="assured">assur'd</span> his welcome, but in the mean while acquainting his Wife, earnestly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="advised">advis'd</span> her to deform her self, what she might, either in dress or otherwise, lest the King, whose amorous inclination was not unknown, should chance to be attracted. She who by this time was not ignorant, how <i>Athelwold</i> had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="stepped">stepd</span> between her and the King, against his coming <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="arrays">arraies</span> her self richly, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="using">useing</span> whatever art she could devise might render her the more amiable; and it took effect. For the King <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="inflamed">inflam'd</span> with her love the more for that he had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="been">bin</span> so long defrauded and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="robbed">rob'd</span> of her, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="resolved">resolv'd</span> not only to recover his intercepted right, but to punish the interloper of his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="desined">destind</span> spouse, and appointing with him as was usual, a day of hunting, drawn aside in a Forest, now <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="called">call'd</span> <i>Harewood</i>, smote him through with a Dart. Some censure this act as cruel and tyrannical, but <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="considered">considerd</span> well, it may be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="judged">judg'd</span> more <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="favorably">favourably</span>, and that no man of sensible Spirit but in his place, without extraordinary perfection, would have done the like: for next to life what worse treason could have <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="been">bin</span> committed against him? it <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="chanced">chanc'd</span> that the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Earl&#x27;s">Earls</span> base Son coming by upon the fact, the King sternly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="asked">ask'd</span> him how he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="liked">lik'd</span> this Game; he submissly answering, that whatsoever <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="pleased">pleas'd</span> the King, must not displease him; the King <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="returned">return'd</span> to his wonted temper, took an affection to the youth, and ever after highly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="favored">favour'd</span> him, making amends in the Son for what he had done to the Father. <i>Elfrida</i> forthwith he took to Wife, who to expiate her former <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Husband&#x27;s">Husbands</span> Death, though <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="therein">therin</span> she had no hand, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="covered">coverd</span> the place of his bloodshed with a Monastery of Nuns to sing over him. [Another fault](note:anotherfault) is laid to his charge, no way excusable, that he took a Virgin <i>Wilfrida</i> by force out of the Nunnery, where she was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="placed">plac'd</span> by her friends to avoid his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="pursuit">persuit</span>, and kept her as his Concubine; but <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="lived">liv'd</span> not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="obstinately">obstinatly</span> in the offence; for sharply <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reproved">reprov'd</span> by <i>Dunstan</i>, he submitted to 7 years penance, and for that time to want his Coronation: But why he had it not before, is left <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="unwritten">unwritt'n</span>. [Another story](note:anotherstoryedgar) there goes of <i>Edgar</i>, fitter for a Novel <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> a History; but as I find it in <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Malmesbury">Malmsbury</span></i>, so I relate it. While he was yet unmarried, in his youth he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="abstained">abstain'd</span> not from Women, and coming on a day to <i>Andover</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="caused">caus'd</span> a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Duke&#x27;s">Dukes</span> Daughter there dwelling, reported rare of Beauty, to be brought to him. The mother not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="daring">dareing</span> flatly to deny, yet abhorring that her Daughter should be so <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="deflowered">deflour'd</span>, at fit time of night sent in her attire, one of her waiting Maids; a Maid it seems not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="unhandsome">unhansome</span> nor unwitty; who <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="supplied">suppli'd</span> the place of her young Lady. Night <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="passed">pass'd</span>, the Maid going to rise, but day-light <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="scarce">scarse</span> yet appearing, was by the King <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="asked">askt</span> why she made such <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="haste">hast</span>; she <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="answered">answer'd</span>, to do the work which her Lady had set her; at which the King <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wondering">wondring</span>, and with much ado staying her to unfold the riddle, for he took her to be the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Duke&#x27;s">Dukes</span> Daughter, she falling at his Feet besought him, that since at the command of her Lady she came to his Bed, and was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="enjoyed">enjoy'd</span> by him, he would be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="pleased">pleas'd</span> in recompence to set her free from the hard service of her Mistress. The King a while standing in a study whether he had best be angry or not, at length turning all to a jest, took the Maid away with him, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="advanced">advanc'd</span> her above her Lady, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="loved">lov'd</span> her, and accompanied with her only, till he married <i>Elfrida</i>. These only are his faults upon record, rather to be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wondered">wonder'd</span> how they were so few, and so soon left, he coming at 16 to the Licence of a Scepter; and that his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="virtues">vertues</span> were so many and so mature, he dying before the Age <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wherein">wherin</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wisdom">wisdome</span> can in others attain to any ripeness: however, with him <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="died">dy'd</span> all the <i>Saxon</i> glory. From henceforth nothing is to be heard of but their decline and ruin under a double Conquest, and the causes foregoing; which, not to blur or taint the praises of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> former actions and liberty well defended, shall stand severally related, and will be more than long enough for another Book.
