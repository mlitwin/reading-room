## The Fifth Book.

</div>

THE <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="sum">summe</span> of things in this <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Island">Iland</span>, or the best part <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="thereof">therof</span>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reduced">reduc't</span> now under the power of one man, and him one of the worthiest, which as far as can be found in good Authors, was by none <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="attained">attain'd</span> at any time <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="here">heer</span> before unless in Fables; men might with some reason have expected from such Union, peace and plenty, greatness, and the flourishing of all Estates and Degrees: but far the contrary fell out soon after, Invasion, Spoil, Desolation, slaughter of many, slavery of the rest, by the forcible landing of a fierce Nation; <i>Danes</i> commonly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="called">call'd</span>, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="sometimes">somtimes</span> <i>Dacians</i>, by others, [the same](note:unsentfor) with <i>Normans</i>; as barbarous as the <i>Saxons</i> themselves were at first reputed, and much more, for the <i>Saxons</i> first invited came hither to dwell; these unsent for, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="unprovoked">unprovok'd</span>, came only to destroy. But if the <i>Saxons</i>, as is above related, came most of them from <i>Jutland</i> and <i>Anglen</i>, a part of <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Denmark">Denmarke</span></i>, as <i>Danish</i> Writers affirm, and that <i>Danes</i> and <i>Normans</i> are the same; then in this invasion, <i>Danes</i> drove out <i>Danes</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own posterity. And <i>Normans</i> afterwards, none but [ancienter](note:ancienter) <i>Normans</i>. Which invasion perhaps, had the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Heptarchy">Heptarchie</span> stood divided as it was, had either not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="been">bin</span> attempted, or not uneasily resisted; while each Prince and people, excited by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="nearest">neerest</span> concernments, had more industriously defended <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own bounds, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> depending on the neglect of a deputed <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Governor">Governour</span>, sent oft-times from the remote residence of a secure Monarch. Though as it fell out in those troubles, the lesser Kingdoms revolting from the <i>West-Saxon</i> yoke, and not aiding each other, too much concerned with <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own safety, it came to no better pass; while severally they sought to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="repel">repell</span> the danger nigh at hand, rather <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> jointly to prevent it <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="far">farre</span> off. But when God hath decreed servitude on a sinful Nation, fitted by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> own vices for no condition but servile, all Estates of Government are alike unable to avoid it. God had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="purposed">purpos'd</span> to punish our instrumental punishers, though now Christians, by other Heathen, according to his Divine retaliation; invasion for invasion, spoil for spoil, destruction for destruction. The <i>Saxons</i> were now full as wicked as the <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Britons">Britans</span></i> were at <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> arrival, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="broken">brok'n</span> with <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="luxury">luxurie</span> and sloth, either secular or superstitious; for laying aside the exercise of Arms, and the study of all <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="virtuous">vertuous</span> knowledge, some betook them to over-worldly or <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="vicious">vitious</span> practice, others to religious Idleness and Solitude, which brought forth nothing but vain and delusive visions; easily <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="perceived">perceav'd</span> such, by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> commanding of things, either not belonging to the Gospel, or utterly forbidden, Ceremonies, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="relics">Reliques</span>, Monasteries, Masses, Idols; add to these ostentation of Alms, got oft-times by rapine and oppression, or intermixt with violent and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="lustful">lustfull</span> deeds, sometimes prodigally <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="bestowed">bestow'd</span> as the expiation of cruelty and bloodshed. What longer suffering could there be, when Religion it self grew so void of sincerity, and the greatest <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="shows">shews</span> of purity were <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="impured">impur'd</span>?
