### <i>Canute</i>, or <i>Knute</i>.

</div>

<i>CAnute</i> having thus [sounded the Nobility](note:1017), and [by them understood](note:bythemunderstood), <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="received">receav'd</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> Oath of fealty, they the pledge of his bare hand, and Oath from the <i>Danish</i> Nobles; <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="whereupon">wherupon</span> the House of <i>Edmund</i> was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="renounced">renounc't</span>, and <i>Canute</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Crowned">Crown'd</span>. Then they enacted, that <i>Edwi</i> Brother of <i>Edmund</i>, a Prince of great hope, should be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="banished">banish't</span> the Realm. But <i>Canute</i> not thinking himself secure while <i>Edwi</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="lived">liv'd</span>, consulted with <i>Edric</i> how to make him away; who told him of one <i>Ethelward</i> a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="decayed">decay'd</span> Nobleman, likeliest to do the work. <i>Ethelward</i> sent for, and tempted by the King in <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="private">privat</span> with largest rewards, but abhorring in his mind the deed, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="promised">promisd</span> to do it when he saw his opportunity; and so still <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="deferred">deferr'd</span> it. But <i>Edwi</i> afterwards <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="received">receav'd</span> into <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="favor">favour</span> as a snare, was by him or some other of his false <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="friends">freinds</span>, <i>Canute</i> contriving it, the same year slain. <i>Edric</i> also <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="counselled">counsel'd</span> him to dispatch <i>Edward</i> and <i>Edmund</i>, the Sons of <i>Ironside</i>; but the King doubting that the fact would seem too foul done in <i>England</i>, sent them to the King of <i>Sweden</i>, with the like intent; but he disdaining the Office, sent them for better safety to <i>Solomon</i> King of <i>Hungary</i>; where <i>Edmund</i> at length dy'd, but <i>Edward</i> married <i>Agatha</i> Daughter to <i>Henry</i> the <i>German</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Emperor">Emperour</span>. A digression in the Laws of <i>Edward</i> Confessor under the Title of <i>Lex Noricorum</i> saith, that this <i>Edward</i> for fear of <i>Canute</i>, fled of his own accord to <i>Malesclot</i> King of the <i>Rugians</i>, who <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="received">receav'd</span> him <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="honorably">honourably</span>, and of that Country [gave him a Wife](note:gavehimawife). <i>Canute</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="settled">settl'd</span> in his Throne, divided the Government of his Kingdom into <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="four">fowr</span> parts; the <i>West-Saxons</i> to himself, the <i>East-Angles</i> to Earl <i>Turkill</i>, the <i>Mercians</i> to <i>Edric</i>, the <i>Northumbrians</i> to <i>Eric</i>; then made peace with all Princes round about him, and his former Wife being dead, in <i>July</i> married <i>Emma</i> the Widow of King <i>Ethelred</i>. The <i>Christmas</i> following was an ill Feast to <i>Edric</i>, of whose Treason, the King having now made use as much as <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="served">serv'd</span> his turn, and fearing himself to be the next <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="betrayed">betray'd</span>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="caused">caus'd</span> him to be slain at <i>London</i> in the Palace, thrown over the City Wall, and there to [lie unburied](note:lieunburied); the head of <i>Edric</i> fixt on a pole, he commanded to be set on the highest Tower of <i>London</i>, as in a double <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="sense">sence</span> he had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="promised">promis'd</span> him, for the murder of King <i>Edmund</i> to exalt him above all the Peers of <i>England</i>. [<i>Huntingdon</i>](note:threeotherswrite), <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Malmesbury">Malmsbury</span></i>, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Matthew">Mat.</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Westminster">West.</span> write, that suspecting the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="King&#x27;s">Kings</span> intention to degrade him from his <i>Mercian</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Dukedom">Dukedome</span>, and upbraiding him with his merits, the King <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="enraged">enrag'd</span>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="caused">caus'd</span> him to be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="strangled">strangl'd</span> in the room, and out at a Window thrown into the <i>Thames</i>. Another writes, that <i>Eric</i> at the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="King&#x27;s">Kings</span> command [struck off](note:struckoff) his head. Other great men, though without fault, as Duke <i>Norman</i> the Son of <i>Leofwin</i>, <i>Ethelward</i> Son of Duke <i>Agelmar</i>, he put to death at the same time, jealous of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> power or familiarity with <i>Edric</i>: and notwithstanding peace, kept still his Army; to maintain which, the [next year](note:1018) he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="squeezed">squees'ed</span> out of the English, though [now his subjects](note:nowhissubjects), not his Enemies, 72, some say, 82 thousand pound, besides 15 thousand out of <i>London</i>. Mean while great War arose at <i>Carr</i>, between <i>Uthred</i> Son of <i>Waldef</i>, Earl of <i>Northumberland</i>, and <i>Malcolm</i> Son of <i>Kened</i> King of <i>Scots</i>, with whom held <i>Eugenius</i> King of <i>Lothian</i>. But <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="here">heer</span> <i>Simeon</i> the relater seems to have committed [some mistake](note:somemistakesimeon), having slain <i>Uthred</i> by <i>Canute</i> two years before, and set <i>Eric</i> in his place: <i>Eric</i> therfore it must needs be, not <i>Uthred</i>, who <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="managed">manag'd</span> this War against the <i>Scots</i>. About which time in a Convention of <i>Danes</i> at <i>Oxford</i>, it was agreed on both parties to keep the Laws of <i>Edgar</i>; <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Matthew">Mat.</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Westminster">West.</span></i> saith of [<i>Edward</i> the Elder](note:1019). The next year <i>Canute</i> [<span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="sailed">sail'd</span> into <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Denmark">Denmarke</span></i>](note:saildinto), and there abode all Winter. <i>Huntingdon</i> and <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Matthew">Mat.</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Westminster">West.</span></i> say, he went <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="thither">thether</span> [to repress](note:torepressswedes) the <i>Swedes</i>; and that the night before a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Battle">Battel</span> to be fought with them, <i>Godwin</i> stealing out of the Camp with his English, assaulted the <i>Swedes</i>, and had got the Victory ere <i>Canute</i> in the morning knew of any fight. For which bold <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="enterprize">enterprise</span>, though against Discipline, he had the English in more esteem ever after. [In the Spring](note:1020) [at his return](note:athisreturn) into <i>England</i>, he held in the time of <i>Easter</i> a great assembly at <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Cirencester">Chirchester</span></i>, and the same year was with <i>Turkill</i> the <i>Dane</i> at the dedication of a Church by them built at <i>Assendune</i>, in the place of that [great Victory](note:assendune) which won him the Crown. But suspecting his greatness, the [year following](note:1021) <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="banished">banish'd</span> him the Realm, and [found occasion](note:foundoccasion) to do the like by <i>Eric</i> the <i>Northumbrian</i> Earl upon the same <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="jealousy">jealousie</span>. Nor yet content with his Conquest of <i>England</i>, though now [above ten years](note:1028) <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="enjoyed">enjoy'd</span>, he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="passed">pass'd</span> with 50 Ships into <i>Norway</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="dispossessed">dispossess'd</span> <i>Olave</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> King, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="subdued">subdu'd</span> the land, first with great <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="sums">summes</span> of money sent the year before to gain him a party, then coming [with an Army](note:1029) to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="compel">compell</span> the rest. [Thence returning](note:thencereturning) King of <i>England</i>, <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Denmark">Denmarke</span></i>, and <i>Norway</i>, yet not secure in his mind, under <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="color">colour</span> of an <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Embassy">Embassey</span> he sent into banishment <i>Hacun</i> a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="powerful">powerfull</span> <i>Dane</i>, who had married the Daughter of his Sister <i>Gunildis</i>, having <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="conceived">conceav'd</span> some <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="suspicion">suspition</span> of his practices against him: but such course was <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="taken">tak'n</span>, that he never came back; either perishing at Sea, or slain by contrivance the [next year](note:1030) in <i>Orkney</i>. <i>Canute</i> [therefore having](note:thereforehaving) thus <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="established">establish't</span> himself by bloodshed and oppression, to [wash away](note:1031), as he thought, the [guilt <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="thereof">therof</span>](note:guilttherof), sailing again into <i>Denmark</i>, went thence to <i>Rome</i>, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="offered">offerd</span> there to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Saint">St.</span> <i>Peter</i> great <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="gifts">guifts</span> of Gold and Silver, and other <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="precious">pretious</span> things; besides the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="usual">usuall</span> tribute of [<i>Romscot</i>](note:greatalms), giving great Alms by the way, both <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="thither">thether</span> and back again, freeing many places of Custom and Toll with great <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="expense">expence</span>, where strangers were wont to pay, having <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="vowed">vow'd</span> great amendment of life at the Sepulchre of <i>Peter</i> and <i>Paul</i>, and to his whole people in a [large letter](note:largeletter) <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="written">writt'n</span> from <i>Rome</i> yet extant. At [his return](note:1032) therefore he built and [dedicated a Church](note:dedicated) to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Saint">St.</span> <i>Edmund</i> at <i>Bury</i>, whom his Ancestors had slain, threw out the secular <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Priests">Preists</span> who had intruded there, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="placed">plac'd</span> Monks in their stead; then going into <i>Scotland</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="subdued">subdu'd</span> and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="received">receav'd</span> [homage of <i>Malcolm</i>](note:homageofmalcolm), and two other Kings there, <i>Melbeath</i> and <i>Jermare</i>. [Three years after](note:1035) having made <i>Swane</i> his [<span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="supposed">suppos'd</span> Son](note:supposdson) by [<i>Algiva</i>](note:algiva) of <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Northampton">Northamton</span></i>, Duke <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Alfhelm&#x27;s">Alfhelms</span></i> Daughter (for others say the Son of a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Priest">Preist</span> whom <i>Algiva</i> barren had got [ready at the time](note:readyatthetime) of her <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="feigned">feign'd</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="labor">labour</span>) King of <i>Norway</i>, and <i>Hardecnute</i> his Son by <i>Emma</i> King of <i>Denmark</i>, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="designed">design'd</span> <i>Harold</i> his Son by <i>Algiva</i> of <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Northampton">Northamton</span></i> King of <i>England</i>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="died">dy'd</span> at <i>Shaftsbury</i>, and [was buried](note:wasburiedat) at <i>Winchester</i> in the old Monastery. This King, as appears, ended better <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> he began; for though he seems to have had no hand in the Death of <i>Ironside</i>, but detested the fact, and bringing the murderers, who came to him in hope of great reward, forth among his Courtiers, as it were to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="receive">receave</span> thanks, after they had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="openly">op'nly</span> related the manner of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> killing him, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="delivered">deliver'd</span> them to deserved punishment, yet he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="spared">spar'd</span> <i>Edric</i> whom he knew to be the prime <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Author">Authour</span> of that detestable fact; till willing to be rid of him, grown importune upon the confidence of his merits, and upbraided by him that he had first <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="relinquished">relinquisht</span>, then <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="extinguished">extinguisht</span> <i>Edmund</i> for his sake; angry to be so upbraided, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="therefore">therfore</span> said he with a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="changed">chang'd</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="countenance">countnance</span>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Traitor">Traytor</span> to God and to me, thou shalt die; thine own mouth accuses thee to have slain thy Master my [confederate Brother](note:confederatebrother), and the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Lord&#x27;s">Lords</span> Anointed. Whereupon although present and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="private">privat</span> Execution was in rage done upon <i>Edric</i>, yet he himself in cool blood <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="scrupled">scrupl'd</span> not to make away the Brother and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Children">Childern</span> of <i>Edmund</i>, who had better right to be the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Lord&#x27;s">Lords</span> Anointed <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="here">heer</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> himself. When he had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="obtained">obtain'd</span> in <i>England</i> what he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="desired">desir'd</span>, no wonder if he sought the love of his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="conquered">conquerd</span> Subjects for the love of his own quiet, the maintainers of his wealth and state for his own profit. For the like reason he is thought to have married <i>Emma</i>, and that <i>Richard</i> Duke of <i>Normandy</i> her Brother might the less care what became of <i>Elfred</i> and <i>Edward</i>, her Sons by King <i>Ethelred</i>. He commanded to be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="observed">observ'd</span> the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="ancient">antient</span> <i>Saxon</i> Laws, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="called">call'd</span> afterwards the Laws of <i>Edward</i> the Confessor, not that <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="he">hee</span> made them, but strictly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="observed">observ'd</span> them. His Letter from <i>Rome</i> professes, if he had done aught amiss in his youth, through negligence or want of due temper, full resolution with the help of God to make amends, by governing justly and piously for the future; charges and adjures all his Officers and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Viscounts">Viscounts</span>, that neither for fear of him, or <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="favor">favour</span> of any person, or to enrich the King, they suffer injustice to be done in the land; commands his treasurers to pay all his Debts ere his return home, which was by <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Denmark">Denmarke</span></i>, to compose matters there; and what his Letter <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="professed">profess'd</span>, he <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="performed">perform'd</span> all his life after. But it is a fond conceit in many great ones, and pernicious in the end, to cease from no violence till they have <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="attained">attain'd</span> the utmost of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> ambitions and desires; then to think God <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="appeased">appeas'd</span> by <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> seeking to bribe him with a share however large of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="ill-gotten">ill-gott'n</span> spoils, and then lastly to grow zealous of doing right, when they have no longer need to do wrong. Howbeit <i>Canute</i> was famous through <i>Europe</i>, and much <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="honored">honour'd</span> of <i>Conrade</i> the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Emperor">Emperour</span>, then at <i>Rome</i>, with rich <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="gifts">guifts</span> and many grants of what he there demanded for the freeing of passages from Toll and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Custom">Custome</span>. I must not omit one remarkable action done by him, as [<i>Huntingdon</i> reports](note:huntingdonreports) it, with great Scene of circumstance, and emphatical expression, to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="show">shew</span> the small power of Kings in respect of God; which, unless to Court-Parasites, needed no such laborious demonstration. He <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="caused">caus'd</span> his Royal Seat to be set on the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="shore">shoar</span>, while the Tide was coming in; and with all the state that Royalty could put into his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="countenance">countnance</span>, said thus to the Sea: Thou Sea belongst to me, and the Land <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="whereon">wheron</span> I sit is mine; nor hath any one <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="unpunished">unpunish't</span> resisted my commands: I charge thee come no <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="further">furder</span> upon my Land, neither presume to wet the Feet of thy <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Sovereign">Sovran</span> Lord. But the Sea, as before, came <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="rolling">rowling</span> on, and without reverence both wet and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="dashed">dash'd</span> him. <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Whereat">Wherat</span> the King quickly <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="rising">riseing</span>, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wished">wish'd</span> all about him to behold and consider the weak and frivolous power of a King, and that none indeed <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="deserved">deserv'd</span> the name of a King, but he whose Eternal Laws, both <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Heaven">Heav'n</span>, Earth, and Sea obey. A truth so evident of it self, as I said before, that unless to shame his Court-Flatterers, who would not else be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="convinced">convinc't</span>, <i>Canute</i> needed not to have gone wet-shod home: The best is, from that time forth he never would wear a Crown, esteeming Earthly Royalty contemptible and vain.
