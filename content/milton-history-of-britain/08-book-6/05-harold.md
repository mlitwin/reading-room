### <i>Harold.</i>

</div>

<i>HArold</i> for his swiftness <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="surnamed">surnam'd</span> [<i>Harefoot</i>](note:hisswiftness), the Son of <i>Canute</i> by <i>Algiva</i> of <i>Northampton</i>, (though some speak doubtfully as if she bore him not, but had him of a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Shoe-maker&#x27;s">Shoo-makers</span> Wife, as <i>Swane</i> before of a <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Priest">Preist</span>; others of a Maid-Servant, to conceal her barrenness) in a great [Assembly at <i>Oxford</i>](note:borehimnot), was by Duke <i>Leofric</i> and the <i>Mercians</i>, with the <i>Londoners</i>, according to his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Father&#x27;s">Fathers</span> Testament, elected King; but [without the Regal Habiliments](note:electedking), which <i>Ælnot</i> the Archbishop having in his Custody, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="refused">refus'd</span> to deliver up, but to the Sons of <i>Emma</i>, for which <i>Harold</i> ever after hated the Clergy; and (as the Clergy are wont thence to <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="infer">inferr</span>) all Religion. <i>Godwin</i> Earl of <i>Kent</i>, and the West-Saxons with him stood for <i>Hardecnute</i>. <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Malmesbury">Malmsbury</span></i> saith, that [the contest was between](note:thecontestwasbetween) Dane and English; that the Danes and Londoners grown now in a manner Danish, were all for <i>Hardecnute</i>; but he being then in <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Denmark">Denmarke</span></i>, <i>Harold</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="prevailed">prevail'd</span>, yet so as that the Kingdom should be divided between them; the West and Southpart <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="reserved">reserv'd</span> by <i>Emma</i> for <i>Hardecnute</i>, till his return. But <i>Harold</i> once <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="advanced">advanc'd</span> into the Throne, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="banished">banish'd</span> <i>Emma</i> his Mother-in law, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="seized">seis'd</span> on his <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Father&#x27;s">Fathers</span> Treasure at <i>Winchester</i>, and there <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="remained">remain'd</span>. <i>Emma</i> [not holding](note:1036) it safe to abide in <i>Normandy</i> while Duke <i>William</i> the Bastard was yet under Age, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="retired">retir'd</span> to <i>Baldwin</i> Earl of <i>Flanders</i>. In [the mean while](note:thebastard) <i>Alfred</i> and <i>Edward</i> Sons of <i>Ethelred</i>, accompanied with a small number of <i>Norman</i> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Soldiers">Souldiers</span> in a few Ships, coming to visit <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> mother <i>Emma</i> not yet departed the land, and perhaps to see how the people were <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="inclined">inclin'd</span> to restore them <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> right; <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Alfred">Elfred</span></i> was sent for by the King then at <i>London</i>; but in his way met at <i>Guilford</i>, by Earl <i>Godwin</i>, who with all seeming <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="friendship">freindship</span> <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="entertained">entertain'd</span> him, was in the night <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="surprised">surpris'd</span> and made <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Prisoner">Prisn'er</span>, most of his Company put to various sorts of cruel Death, decimated twice over; then brought to <i>London</i>, was by the King sent bound to <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Ely">Eely</span></i>, had his Eyes put out by the way, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="delivered">deliverd</span> to the Monks there, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="died">dy'd</span> soon after in <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> Custody. <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Malmesbury">Malmsbury</span></i> gives [little credit](note:littlecredit) to this story of <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Alfred">Elfred</span></i>, as not <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Chronicled">Chronicl'd</span> in his time, but <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="rumored">rumour'd</span> only. Which <i>Emma</i> however hearing, sent away her Son <i>Edward</i>, who by good hap accompanied not his Brother, with all speed into <i>Normandy</i>. But the <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Author">Authour</span> of <i>Encomium Emmæ</i>, who seems plainly (though nameless) to have <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="been">bin</span> some Monk, yet <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="lived">liv'd</span>, and perhaps wrote within the same year when these things were done; by [his relation](note:byhisrelation) differing from all others, much aggravates the cruelty of <i>Harold</i>, that he not content to have <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="practiced">practis'd</span> in secret (for <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="openly">op'nly</span> he durst not) against the life of <i>Emma</i>, sought many treacherous ways to get her Son within his power; and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="resolved">resolv'd</span> at length to forge a Letter in the name of <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> Mother, inviting them into <i>England</i>, the Copy of which Letter he produces <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="written">writt'n</span> to this purpose.

<i><span class="roman">EMma</span> in name only Queen, to her Sons <span class="roman">Edward</span> and <span class="roman"><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Alfred">Alfrid</span></span> imparts motherly salutation. While we severally bewail the Death of our Lord the King, most Dear Sons, and while daily yee are <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="deprived">depriv'd</span> more and more of the Kingdom your Inheritance; I admire what Counsel yee take, knowing that your intermitted delay is a daily strengthning to the Reign of your Usurper, who incessantly goes about from Town to City, gaining the Chief Nobles to his party, either by gifts, prayers, or threats. But they had much rather one of you should reign over them, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="than">then</span> to be held under the power of him who now over-rules them. I entreat therefore that one of you come to me speedily, and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="privately">privatly</span>, to receive from me <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="wholesome">wholesom</span> Counsel, and to know how the business which I intend shall be <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="accomplished">accomplisht</span>. By this Messenger present, send back what you determine. Farewell, as dear both as my own Heart.</i>

These Letters were sent to the Princes then in <i>Normandy</i>, by express Messengers, with presents also as from <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="their">thir</span> mother; which they joyfully receiving, return word by the same Messengers, that one of them will be with her shortly; naming both the time and place. <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Alfred">Alfrid</span></i> therefore the younger (for so it was thought best) at the appointed time, with a few Ships and small numbers about him appearing on the Coast, no sooner came ashore but fell into the snare of Earl <i>Godwin</i>, sent on purpose to betray him; as above was related. <i>Emma</i> greatly sorrowing for the loss of her Son, thus cruelly made away, fled <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="immediately">immediatly</span> with some of the Nobles her faithfullest adherents into <i>Flanders</i>, had her dwelling <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="assigned">assign'd</span> at <i>Bruges</i> by the Earl; where having <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="remained">remain'd</span> about [two years](note:1039), she was visited out of <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Denmark">Denmarke</span></i> by <i>Hardecnute</i> [her Son](note:herson); and he not long had <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="remained">remain'd</span> with her there, when <i>Harold</i> in <i>England</i>, having [done nothing](note:donenothing) the while worth memory, save [the taxing](note:1040) of every Port at 8 marks of Silver to 16 Ships, <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="died">dy'd</span> at <i>London</i>, [some say](note:somesayat) at <i>Oxford</i>; and was buried at <i>Winchester</i>. After which, most of the Nobility, both Danes and English now agreeing, send <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Ambassadors">Embassadors</span> to <i>Hardecnute</i> still at <i>Bruges</i> with his mother, entreating him to come and <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="receive">receave</span> as his right the Scepter, who before <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Midsummer">Midsomer</span> came with 60 Ships, and many <span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Soldiers">Souldiers</span> out of <i><span class="inline-gloss" data-gloss="Denmark">Denmarke</span></i>.
