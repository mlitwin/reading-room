---
title: Integration
---

Integration on manifolds acts on top-degree forms over oriented manifolds. A metric adds a canonical top form, the volume form, so functions can be integrated too. The capstone is [Stokes's theorem](note:stokess-theorem), $\int_M d\tens{\omega} = \int_{\partial M} \tens{\omega}$, which contains the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's, Gauss's and the classical Stokes theorems.

Three pages:

1. [Orientation and integration](01-orientation-and-integration.md): orientations, integrals of $n$-forms, change of variables.
2. [Volume form and Hodge star](02-volume-form-and-hodge-star.md): $\mathrm{vol}_g$, integrals of functions, divergence, the area of $S^2$, the star and vector calculus.
3. [Stokes's theorem](03-stokes-theorem.md): manifolds with boundary, the theorem, the divergence theorem.

**Metric-free:** orientation, integration of forms and Stokes's theorem. The volume form, divergence and Hodge star need $\tens{g}$.

**Where this lands in GR.** The volume form makes the Einstein–Hilbert action $\int_M (R - 2\Lambda)\, \mathrm{vol}_g$ a well-defined number. Stokes's theorem is behind the integration by parts in every variational derivation and behind conserved-charge integrals over spatial slices.
