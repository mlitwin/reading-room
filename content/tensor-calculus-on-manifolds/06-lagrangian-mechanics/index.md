---
title: Lagrangian mechanics
---

A mechanical system is a [configuration space](note:configuration-space) $Q$, an $n$-manifold of positions, together with a Lagrangian on its tangent bundle $TQ$, the space of positions and velocities. The physical motions are the stationary points of the [action](note:functional-and-variation). Nothing in this chapter needs a metric: it uses the manifold, the tangent bundle, and 1-forms from Part I.

Three pages:

1. [Newtonian mechanics](01-newtonian-mechanics.md): the flat special case, with forces read as 1-forms and the classical conservation laws.
2. [Action and the Euler–Lagrange equations](02-action-and-euler-lagrange.md): Hamilton's principle, the equations of motion, and why they are tensorial.
3. [Constraints and examples](03-constraints-and-examples.md): holonomic constraints as submanifolds, multipliers, cyclic coordinates, the pendulum, and the particle on $S^2$.

**Where this lands in GR.** Free fall in general relativity is the Euler–Lagrange equation of the Lagrangian $\tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}\, \dot x^\mu \dot x^\nu$ on spacetime ([chapter 12](../12-connection-and-curvature/02-covariant-derivative.md)). That is the same machinery with a four-dimensional Lorentzian configuration space.
